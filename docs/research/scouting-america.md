## 1. History and the 2024/2025 Rebrand

*This section was verified line-by-line against the official press release and the national explainer, and holds up. Two dates were sharpened.*

- **May 7, 2024** — The Boy Scouts of America announced it would rebrand its corporate/organizational name to **Scouting America**. Units were told to adopt the new name right away rather than wait: *"Though the name change doesn't become official until Feb. 8, 2025, the new name should be used immediately."* ([Scouting Newsroom press release](https://www.scoutingnewsroom.org/press-releases/boy-scouts-of-america-to-become-scouting-america/), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/boy-scouts-of-america-to-become-scouting-america-302137787.html), [onscouting.org explainer](https://onscouting.org/2024/05/07/what-our-organizations-name-change-means-and-doesnt-mean-to-scouts-and-leaders/)) ✅ *verified*
- **February 8, 2025** — The name change became **official**, timed to coincide with the organization's **115th anniversary** (BSA was founded Feb 8, 1910). ✅ *verified*
- **Why**: The organization said the change reflects its ongoing commitment to welcoming *every* youth and family in America into Scouting. Quote from President/CEO Roger A. Krone: *"Though our name will be new, our mission remains unchanged: we are committed to teaching young people to be Prepared. For Life."* ✅ *verified verbatim*
  - ⚠️ **Corrected date:** an earlier draft of this doc attributed the girls' admission to "the 2019 decision." The BSA national board voted to open all programs to girls on **October 11, 2017**; **Cub Scouting** opened to girls during **2018**; **Scouts BSA** opened **February 1, 2019**. The 2024 press release frames the rebrand around the *fifth anniversary* of that last milestone.
- **Key stats cited in the announcement**: 176,000+ girls/young women served, 6,000+ girls earning Eagle Scout, 1M+ total youth participants, 477,000 adult volunteers. ✅ *all four verified verbatim against the press release*
- **What did NOT change**: Program names remain exactly as before — **Cub Scouting, Scouts BSA, Venturing, Sea Scouts, and Exploring** all keep their existing names. There is **no new acronym** — national guidance is *"we're encouraging everyone to call the program simply Scouting America"* (avoid inventing "SA" as shorthand). "Scouting" replaces "Boy Scouts" as the umbrella/corporate term; the Scout Oath, Scout Law, mission, and Duty to God are unchanged. ✅ *verified*
- Program-level visual identity (the Cub Scouts trademark, the Scouts BSA emblem) was **largely retained**, but "only the corporate wordmark changed" is too strong. Two concrete changes: ([Great Falls Council](https://gfcscouting.org/scouting-name-change/), [freedomandglory.com summary](https://www.freedomandglory.com/blog/the-boy-scouts-of-america-is-rebranding-to-scouting-america-what-you-need-to-know))
  - The 2024 Brand Guidelines retire a mark outright: *"The gold fleur-de-lis has been retired and should not be used."* (p.10) — **do not use a gold/single-color-gold fleur-de-lis anywhere on the site.**
  - Uniforms gained *"new uniforms with 'Scouting America' lettering over the right pocket. The rest of the uniform will remain unchanged."*
- **Naming convention takeaway for a pack site**: Refer to the parent organization as "Scouting America" (not "the Boy Scouts" / "BSA" in new copy, though BSA still appears in older legal/registration contexts like myscouting.org). Refer to the local program correctly as "**Cub Scout Pack 170**" or "**Pack 170, Scouting America**" — not "Boy Scout Pack."

### Typography

> ⚠️ **This subsection was wrong and has been rewritten.** The original presented a single flat list of eight-plus typefaces as "the BSA fonts." The source sheet actually lists **three separate brand systems side by side**, and the original list merged them, then guessed that the system fonts were "likely fallback/legacy." Both moves were mistakes. Corrected below from the 2024 Brand Guidelines p.16 and the Dec 2025 color/typeface sheet.

**The Scouting America approved typefaces are exactly four.** Guidelines p.16: *"The fonts below are approved for use by Scouting America. They are widely available typefaces. For consistency across the organization, please use this suite of fonts as your foundation for any branded communication. Although additional fonts may be used as design elements, the foundation of any piece must be rooted in the fonts below."*

| Typeface | Role | Availability |
|---|---|---|
| **Proxima Nova Extra Bold** | Display / headline | Licensed — Adobe Creative Cloud or other vendors |
| **Helvetica Neue LT Std 77 Bold Condensed** | Display / condensed | Licensed |
| **Arial** | Universal / body | System font |
| **Times New Roman** | Universal / body | System font |

⚠️ **Arial and Times New Roman are first-class approved brand fonts, not "fallbacks."** The original doc demoted them to "legacy system fonts for documents"; the guidelines list them alongside Proxima Nova with equal standing. *(Trebuchet also appears, but only in the email-signature spec on p.17 — not a general brand font.)*

**Montserrat is the officially designated free substitute — not merely "a reasonable one."** Guidelines p.16, verbatim: *"The Proxima Nova type family is a licensed font family available with an Adobe Creative Cloud subscription or through other vendors. **An approved alternate from Google fonts is Montserrat.**"* This is the single most useful typography finding for the pack site: **Montserrat for headlines is officially sanctioned, free, and self-hostable.** No license to buy, no compromise.

**Fonts that are NOT Scouting America fonts** — these appear on the same summary sheet but belong to other brand systems, and using them will make the site look like the wrong organization:

| Typeface | Actually belongs to |
|---|---|
| Museo Slab 700/300, Museo Sans 700/300, Rockwell, Gill Sans Light | **Order of the Arrow** |
| Futura Bold / Futura, Verdana / Verdana Bold | **World Scouting (WOSM)** |

⚠️ **Removed as unverifiable:** the original list included **Gill Sans Nova** and **Calibri**. Neither appears on any official sheet — the OA system uses plain Gill Sans Light, and Calibri appears nowhere at all.

### Logo usage / trademark restrictions relevant to a pack site

> 🔁 **SUPERSEDED — read [`trademark-brand-guidance.md`](./trademark-brand-guidance.md) instead.** That document answers this question from primary sources and **reverses two recommendations made here.** This subsection is kept only as a pointer; do not act on the original text, which is retained nowhere below. The corrected summary:

- All Scouting America emblems, rank insignia, logos, and trademarks are the **exclusive property of Scouting America**, protected by the **1916 congressional charter plus USPTO registrations**. ⚠️ **Cite correction:** the current codification is **36 U.S.C. § 30905**, not the "36 U.S.C. 27" printed in the brand guidelines themselves (that is the pre-1998 cite). ⚠️ **Overstatement corrected:** this is *not* uniquely "one of the strongest trademark protections of any US nonprofit" — roughly **115 Title 36 federally chartered organizations** hold comparable rights. It is unusually strong, but not singular.
- Official art obtained from the National Council **must be used exactly as shown**. The prohibition is on **modification, not on use**.
- ⚠️ **REVERSED:** the original recommended using *"generic/decorative scouting-style iconography (compass rose, trail markers)"* as the "legally safer" path. National brand policy says the opposite — legacy Guidelines p.59: *"As a rule, avoid creating original icons. Instead, use approved Boy Scouts of America icons (e.g., the fleur-de-lis as an app icon)."* **Using the real, unmodified fleur-de-lis is the recommended route, not the risky one.**
- ⚠️ **REVERSED:** the original said *"treat logo acquisition as 'ask your council' rather than 'download and use.'"* The **Brand Center exists specifically so units can download and use official artwork** — *"search, download, and customize materials for your pack, troop, or post."* Register and download; you do not need to ask permission first. (Separately, the Central Georgia Council publishes **no** brand guidance at all, so "ask your council" was a dead end regardless.)
- **The rule that actually bites** is on physical goods, not the website: anything printed on a product (T-shirts, patches, banners) bearing a Scouting mark **must be made by a licensed vendor**. See `trademark-brand-guidance.md` §6.

## 3. Visual Identity Themes

- **The fleur-de-lis**: Scouting's founder Robert Baden-Powell adopted it for its use on maps and compasses to indicate north. ⚠️ **Quote correction:** *"it shows the true way to go"* is a paraphrase, not B-P's words, and was presented as a quotation in the original. What B-P actually wrote is that the badge *"points in the right direction (and upwards), turning neither to the right nor left, since these lead backward again,"* and in *Scouting for Boys* (1908), *"The Scout's badge is the arrow head, which shows the north on a map or on the compass."*
  - In the American adaptation the three points stand for **the three parts of the Scout Oath** — duty to God and country, duty to others, and duty to self. ✅ *verified* (B-P's original 1908 gloss referred to the three points of the Scout **Promise**: duty to God and the king, helpfulness to others, obedience to the Scout Law.)
  - The two five-pointed stars represent **truth and knowledge**, and their **ten points** echo the ten points of the Scout Law. ✅ *verified* — ⚠️ but note the stars are **not** part of the bare fleur-de-lis. Per the 2024 Guidelines, they belong to the composite emblems: the corporate trademark *"consist[s] of a fleur-de-lis with eagle, shield with 13 stars and stripes, and two five-pointed stars"* (p.10), as does the Scouts BSA trademark (p.25). The **Cub Scouts** trademark is different again — *"the wolf, the words 'Cub Scouts,' and a fleur-de-lis"* — and has no stars.
  - ([ScoutSmarts history](https://scoutsmarts.com/fleur-de-lis-scouts-history/), [Scouting Magazine — three points to know about the fleur-de-lis](https://onscouting.org/2021/01/13/sports-history-scouting-and-beyond-three-points-to-know-about-the-fleur-de-lis/) — ⚠️ *URL updated; the old `blog.scoutingmagazine.org` address now 301-redirects to `onscouting.org`*)
- **Cub Scout blue and gold**: the defining color pair for the Cub Scout program specifically (distinct from the Scouts BSA tan/olive/red palette). This is *the* motif to lean on for a Cub Scout pack site — it's why the pack's big annual dinner is called the "**Blue and Gold Banquet**." ⚠️ **Correction:** the original called this a *"fundraising/recognition dinner."* It is not a fundraiser. It is the **February celebration of Scouting's birthday** (Feb 8, 1910), normally replacing the regular February pack meeting, and is named after the Cub Scout colors. The name entered BSA literature in **1943**.
- **Rank insignia aesthetics**: Cub Scout ranks use bold, simple, high-contrast badge/shield shapes with embroidered-patch styling — thick borders, saturated single-color fields, iconic animal silhouettes. This "patch" aesthetic is a strong motif to borrow for section dividers and achievement-style flourishes.
  - ⚠️ **Rank list updated for the June 1, 2024 Cub Scout program overhaul.** Current ranks are **Lion** (K), **Tiger** (1st), **Wolf** (2nd), **Bear** (3rd), **Webelos** (4th), **Arrow of Light** (5th). Two changes the site's copy must reflect: **Arrow of Light is now a stand-alone 5th-grade rank**, no longer part of Webelos; and **Bobcat is no longer a rank** — it is now the first required adventure within *every* rank. A site that describes "Webelos/Arrow of Light" as one thing, or lists Bobcat as a joining rank, is describing the pre-2024 program.
  - On *displaying* official rank badge artwork versus inventing lookalikes: see [`trademark-brand-guidance.md`](./trademark-brand-guidance.md) §5.5. Short version — displaying official artwork is fine and near-universal; **manufacturing** anything resembling rank insignia is definitively banned.
- General Scouting visual vocabulary: khaki/olive textures, trail/compass iconography, wood-badge/camp aesthetic, embroidered patch borders — useful as supporting texture rather than primary brand carriers.

## 4. Official Web Resources

- **scouting.org** — the national public-facing site (news, program info, Guide to Safe Scouting, Youth Protection resources, legal/privacy policy).
- **scoutingwire.org** — the internal-facing "council/unit leader" resource hub, hosting the **BSA Brand Center** overview and links into the gated Brand Center DAM (`scouting.webdamdb.com`) where official logo files, templates, and the full Brand Identity Guide PDF live. Registration/login required for full access.
- **scoutingnewsroom.org** — press releases (rebrand announcement, etc.).
- Local councils generally mirror brand assets on their own sites (e.g., Capitol Area Council, Tidewater Council, Pathway to Adventure Council) — useful fallback if the national Brand Center is inaccessible, since they often re-host the same PDFs publicly. ⚠️ *Note: the **Central Georgia Council** — Pack 170's own council — publishes none of this. See [`trademark-brand-guidance.md`](./trademark-brand-guidance.md) §4.1.*
- **[beascout.scouting.org](https://beascout.scouting.org/)** — ⚠️ *added during fact-check; missing from the original.* Every unit has a **BeAScout "unit pin"**, an official, national, public-facing listing with the pack's meeting location, contact, and a join link. This is the closest thing to an official pack web presence, it is the front door families actually arrive through, and **it is maintained by unit Key 3 leaders via my.scouting.org**. macon170.com should link to it and the pack should keep the pin's details in sync with the site.
- **No official "unit website builder" or hosted template system exists** — confirmed. Units build their own or use third-party services. Guidance is folded into the legacy Brand Guidelines' **Web Policies** chapter (p.43) plus the **Social Media Guidelines**. ✅ *verified; note that national has run features showcasing the best pack/troop/crew websites, so unit-run sites are plainly normal and encouraged rather than merely tolerated.*

## 5. Youth Protection / Privacy Considerations for Unit Websites

This is the most operationally important section for building the pack site — get this wrong and it's a real policy/legal problem, not just a style nit.

- **National privacy policy does not cover unit sites — but check the wording.** ⚠️ **Correction:** the original said the policy *"explicitly states it does not apply to individual council or unit websites."* It says no such thing about **units**. What it actually says: *"'we', 'us' or 'our' refers to the Boy Scouts of America National Council and its affiliated entities, **not including our local councils**,"* plus *"The information you provide to BSA local councils through their websites and applications is collected separately by them and is subject to their privacy practices."* Units are never mentioned — they are a further step removed, so **the conclusion stands a fortiori: a pack site needs its own privacy policy.** Just don't cite the national policy as if it names units. ([privacy policy](https://www.scouting.org/legal/privacy-policy/) — ⚠️ *returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching; verified via the Internet Archive*)
  - ⚠️ **Removed as unsupported:** the original also claimed a pack site *"needs to name a responsible admin/webmaster."* Nothing in the privacy policy requires this. The nearest real requirement is the legacy Brand Guidelines' website **Required Elements** (p.73): **clear contact information on every page** plus a **copyright notice in the footer**. Cite that instead.
  - **Better source for the actual obligation** — the national [Digital Safety page](https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/safety-moments/digital-safety-and-online-scouting-activities/) states it directly: *"If you collect a person's personal information online—for example, through web forms…—then you should post a notice or disclosure at the point of collection describing how you will use the information. The notice should be conspicuous and written in plain English."*
- **COPPA applies — and there is a verbatim national rule for it.** ✅ *Upgraded from paraphrase to primary source.* From the Digital Safety page: *"**Do not collect personal information directly from youth under 13 years of age** due to the parental notice and consent requirements under the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act ('COPPA'). **You should collect any data needed from the parent or legal guardian only.**"* Also: *"Collecting personal information from youth under 13 is not recommended."* Route every form through a parent/guardian.
- **Names — first name and last initial.** ✅ **Upgraded from "historical guidance" to a hard, written rule.** The original hedged this as informal carryover practice. It is printed verbatim in the official Brand Guidelines web policy (p.43): ***"When identifying youth members on your website, use first name and last initial only. Don't provide too much identifiable information."*** Treat it as binding. Never publish a minor's last name, home address, phone number, school name/location, or parents' workplace. The Digital Safety page reinforces the principle: *"keep such information private and not share a youth's personal information with anyone except that youth's parent or guardian or the unit leader responsible for tracking advancements."*
- **Photos require permission — but "explicit, signed consent" overstates the national rule.** ⚠️ **Softened during fact-check.** No national document found imposes a *signed* release as a precondition for publishing. What the sources actually say:
  - Brand Guidelines p.43 (a council rule that binds a pack independently via Youth Protection): sites must *"consider the safety and privacy of their members and participants by **obtaining the necessary permissions** to release information about or images of any individual."*
  - Social Media Guidelines: *"all videos/images should adhere to recommended Youth Protection policies and should protect the privacy of individual Scouts"* — this document does **not** itself mandate parental permission.
  - [Scouting Magazine's social-media safety piece](https://scoutingmagazine.org/2021/05/avoid-social-media-safety-pitfalls/) is advisory: *"Have you asked the kids' parents if they're OK with you sharing photos of their children? If the answer is 'I'm not sure,' then refrain from posting."* It also recommends omitting kids' names from captions, avoiding location tags, and deleting posted images on request.
  - **Practical read:** a signed photo release on the pack's annual registration paperwork is the standard, sensible *mechanism* for "obtaining the necessary permissions," and remains the right thing for Pack 170 to do — but present it as pack policy and prudent practice, not as a quoted national mandate. ([Coosa District Social Media guide](https://nwgabsa.doubleknot.com/document/coosa-district-media-guide/155245) ✅ *live*)
- **Public, not private, channels.** ✅ *verified verbatim.* Accounts must designate *"at least two administrators who have access to the login, password, and page management/monitoring information,"* at least one being a Scouting America employee, council employee, or **registered volunteer with Youth Protection training**. All channels must be public: *"Private channels and private communication put both the youth and you at risk"* — no private Facebook groups, no invite-only YouTube channels. ([Scouting Wire Social Media Guidelines](https://scoutingwire.org/social-media-guidelines/) ✅ *live*; [PDF, Feb 2025](https://tidewaterbsa.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Scouting-America-Social-Media-Guidelines-021525.pdf))
  - ⚠️ *Scope note:* these rules are written for **social media accounts**, and the original doc applied them to "any unit web/social presence" without saying so. The extension to a pack website is sound (a website is even more public than a page) but is an inference, not the document's literal scope.
- **No one-on-one adult-youth contact in any medium.** ✅ *verified verbatim.* Social Media Guidelines: *"no private messages and no one-on-one direct contact through email, Facebook messages, Twitter direct messaging, chats, instant messaging"*; any adult-youth electronic communication must have *"one or more authorized adults openly 'copied' (included) on the message."* Digital Safety page: *"Our ban on one-on-one contact between an adult leader and youth applies to all interactions – whether in person, online, through a web conference, over the phone, via text, or in any other form."* If the site has a "contact a leader" form or chat feature, it must not create a private adult–youth channel.
- **Digital safety extends the in-person rules.** ✅ *verified verbatim.* Digital Safety page: *"All youth protection policies still apply in an online environment. Ensure you always have two-deep leadership for online activities and meetings."* Also *"All aspects of the Scouting program are open to observation by parents."* ([Scouting America Digital Safety page](https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/safety-moments/digital-safety-and-online-scouting-activities/) — ⚠️ *returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching; verified via the Internet Archive*)
- **Do not record online meetings with youth.** ⚠️ *Missing from the original; added.* Digital Safety page: *"Do **not** record online activities/meetings that include youth participants."* Relevant if the site ever hosts or embeds recorded pack meetings.
- ⚠️ **Unlocated resource:** the Digital Safety page's Resources list references a *"BSA's Digital Privacy guidelines"* document alongside the Social Media Guidelines. No public copy of it could be found at any scouting.org path or council mirror. If the pack wants the most authoritative statement on unit-site privacy, this is the document to request from the council.

### Design/compliance checklist this implies for macon170.com
- Publish a pack-specific privacy policy (don't rely on or claim the national one), and post a plain-English notice at the point of collection on any form.
- Any registration or "contact us" form should be addressed to a parent/guardian, not framed as youth-facing data collection. Collect nothing directly from a child under 13.
- Photo galleries: **first name and last initial at most** (this is a written rule, not a nicety) — or no names at all, just group/event captions. Only publish photos the pack has permission to publish.
- No embedded youth-only chat/DM feature; keep any contact mechanism public or routed to multiple adults.
- Clear contact information on every page, and a copyright notice in the footer (Brand Guidelines "Required Elements," p.73).
- A photo opt-out mechanism for families who decline the release, consistent with how the pack collects consent on paper.
- Don't post recordings of pack meetings or online activities that include youth.
- No ads, no commercial endorsements, no selling Scouting-branded merchandise on the site — which also rules out ad-injecting free hosting tiers.
