# Pitching a Cub Scout Pack to Bibb County School District: Synthesis

Compiled 2026-07-27, drawing on five research notes in `notes/`. Revised 2026-07-27 after a
client correction to the ask and the framing (see `README.md` for what changed). Written for
whoever carries this into a room with a BCSD principal, a PTA/PTO board, or the Office of
Partnerships, and to inform the public web page this research will eventually support.

Evidence labels used throughout: `[peer-reviewed]`, `[organization-commissioned]`,
`[district's own document]`, `[argument — no direct evidence]`. The label follows the
specific claim it supports, not the section as a whole.

This document is written to work at any Bibb County elementary school, not only Pack 170's home
school. The Alexander II section below is the pack's own worked example, not the subject of the
whole pitch. Sections marked "district-general" apply the same way anywhere in BCSD.

---

## The real ask, stated up front

Pack 170 is not asking a school for meeting space, a room, or a standing program the school has
to run or supervise. **The ask is a table at family events already on the school's calendar** —
an open house, a fall festival, a family night — plus, as lighter secondary asks, a few minutes
on a PTO meeting agenda or permission to post a flyer. Everything else in this document is either
the case for why that small ask fits what the district says it wants (the alignment case, which
is the actual content of the eventual public web page) or backup for the handful of questions an
administrator will have before saying yes. See "The practical ask" below for the specifics.

---

## The audience and what they fear — and who is actually asking

Three different readers will see this pitch, and they worry about different things. Before
getting to their specific worries, one framing correction matters more than any of them: **Pack
170 is not an outside organization asking for entry.** Most of the children in the pack already
attend the school in question (Alexander II, in the worked example below), and many of the pack's
adults are already approved BCSD school volunteers under Regulation IFCD-R for other reasons —
classroom help, field trips, and similar. This is the pack's own background, not a claim sourced
to a BCSD document, and it does not by itself resolve the one open procedural question in this
pitch (see "The practical ask"). But it changes the shape of several objections below: this is not
"who are these people," it is "here is a piece of our own parent community, organized."

That said, the research should not assume a warm reception. **Assume an administrator with no
prior relationship to the pack, who has no reason to extend trust yet and a real cost to
defending an unusual decision to a supervisor or to other parents.** That assumption, not any
claim about a specific person's temperament, should drive the strategy below — it holds at every
school in the district, whether or not the pack has a history there.

**A principal** worries about time and liability. A group asking for a room, a key, and a slot on
the calendar looks, from a building-level view, like one more thing that can go wrong: an
unsupervised adult near children, a safety incident, a program that fizzles after one semester and
leaves a gap. A single-day table at an event the school already runs, staffed by named,
already-vetted adults, does not carry the same weight — but an administrator still has to be able
to explain the decision if asked, which is why the formal-channel questions below matter (see
"What a cautious administrator is protecting against").

**The Office of Partnerships** worries about fit and reputation. Their own definition of a
partner explicitly excludes "organizations or groups seeking promotional access to students" and
"visitors who attend without meaningful interaction" `[district's own document]` (source:
`notes/strategic-plan.md`, quoting
[bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/whatisapartner](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/whatisapartner)).
They also carry institutional memory of Scouting America's abuse history and bankruptcy — if they
know that history, they will wonder whether hosting a table exposes the district to reputational
risk. That has to be answered directly, not avoided (see the risk register below).

**A PTA/PTO board** worries about competition: for volunteer hours, for family attention, for the
modest sponsorship dollars local businesses give schools. They will ask whether a table duplicates
something they already run, or whether it draws parents away from existing commitments (room
parent, booster club, Title I council). For a table at a PTO-run event, this board, not the
principal, may be the actual decision-maker — see "Who decides" under "The practical ask."

The throughline: none of these three readers is primarily persuaded by data about Scouting's
benefits to a child. They are persuaded by evidence that a table costs them nothing in time, risk,
or precedent, and that saying yes is not a personal judgment call they have to defend alone.

## The opening

BCSD's 2023–2028 strategic plan, #Built4Bibb, states its own Theory of Action in these
words: _"if we build stakeholder engagement of students, parents staff and community, and
strengthen staff effectiveness to drive teaching and learning, we will maximize student
achievement."_ `[district's own document]` (source:
[bcsdk12.net/about-us/strategic-plan](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/strategic-plan), via
`notes/strategic-plan.md`). Stakeholder engagement is not one goal among three of equal
weight — it is the causal input the district's own plan says everything else depends on.

Goal 3 is that engagement goal, with three sub-goals and a 2022–23 school-year baseline
against 2028 targets, published in the district's "Specific Performance Measures" document
`[district's own document]`
([B4BMVPPerformanceMeasures.pdf](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1717691735/bibbk12gaus/bpne7zajzzvwb4kq5mre/B4BMVPPerformanceMeasures.pdf)):

- **% students committed to "Show Up": 33% baseline → 95% target.** This is the single
  largest self-identified gap of any Goal 3 metric with a real baseline — a 62-point spread.
  Staff are already near target (87% → 95%); students and families are not.
- **# business/community stakeholders/partners actively engaged: 337 → 1,000.** A
  countable head-count metric.
- **% parents/guardians volunteering: TBD baseline → 70% target.**
- **% parents/guardians attending academic school meetings per year: TBD → 80%.**
- **% students participating in extra-curricular activities: TBD → 90%.**

All figures carry the 2022–23 school year as their baseline year; several targets were
still "TBD" (not yet measured) as of the plan's June 2023 approval, and no later public
progress report against these specific numbers could be located (`notes/strategic-plan.md`)
— see Open Questions below. The metrics named above are the ones a pack's families plausibly
move: a parent volunteer, a parent at a meeting, a child in an extracurricular, a family that
shows up. No other Goal 3 or Goal 1 metric has as direct a mechanical link to what pack families
already do. This is a district-general point: it does not depend on which school is asked.

## Goal 3 alignment (district-general)

### 3.1 — "Develop a culture of collective ownership"

District wording, quoted exactly: _"Develop a culture of collective ownership."_
`[district's own document]`. Measures include the partner-count metric above (337 → 1,000)
and _"% stakeholders committed to 'Show Up'"_ broken out by students (33% → 95%), staff
(87% → 95%), and parents (TBD → 80%).

What pack families concretely are: families already showing up at a school, month after month,
whether or not the pack itself is formally registered as anything with the district. A table at a
family event and a listing in the Office of Partnerships' partner categories (if the pack later
wants one — see "The practical ask") are two different-weight ways of making that visible, but the
underlying fact — recurring family engagement — exists either way.

Evidence strength: the partner-count mechanism is `[district's own document]` — it is
literally how the district counts, if a pack chooses to register formally. The claim that pack
families measurably narrow the 33%→95% Show-Up gap is `[argument — no direct evidence]`: no
source ties Scouting specifically to this metric; it is an inference from the size of the gap, not
a district or research finding (`notes/strategic-plan.md`).

### 3.2 — "Develop mutual trust and relationships with and amongst stakeholder groups"

District wording, quoted exactly: _"Develop mutual trust and relationships with and amongst
stakeholder groups."_ `[district's own document]`. Measures: _"% of parents/guardians
volunteering"_ (TBD → 70%), _"% of parents/guardians attending academic school meetings per
year"_ (TBD → 80%), and student/staff engagement indices (3.83 → 4.95 and 4.04 → 4.95; survey
scale not documented in the source).

What pack families concretely are: every den requires an adult volunteer under Scouting
America's two-deep leadership rule, and that adult must complete a criminal background check and
Youth Protection Training before serving `[organization-commissioned/primary-document]` — this is
the same vetting category BCSD itself requires of any campus volunteer under Board Regulation
IFCD-R (`notes/district-profile.md`,
[bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteer](https://www.bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteer)). At a school where
pack families already attend, some of these adults are already on file as approved BCSD
volunteers for other reasons — which strengthens, not weakens, this point: these are exactly the
engaged, already-vetted parents the district says it wants more of.

Evidence strength: the requirement that pack adults be vetted volunteers is
`[district's own document]` plus Scouting America's own primary policy documents. The claim
that this specific population _increases_ the district's measured "% parents volunteering" is
`[argument — no direct evidence]` — no study measures whether Scout-parent volunteering predicts
subsequent school volunteering (`notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §5.3). The general research
base that family involvement correlates with school outcomes — Henderson & Mapp (2002), a
synthesis of 50+ studies — is `[peer-reviewed]`, but it is correlational, not Scouting-specific,
and does not examine youth organizations at all (`notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §5.1).

### 3.3 — "Strengthen practices of engagement to maximize students' overall learning experiences"

District wording, quoted exactly: _"Strengthen practices of engagement to maximize
students' overall learning experiences."_ `[district's own document]`. Measures include
_"% of students participating in extra-curricular activities"_ (TBD → 90%) and _"# of
established internships with business/community partners per high school"_ (TBD → 100 —
worded for high schools, a weak fit for a K-5 pack).

What pack families concretely are: children already in an extracurricular activity that a
school could, if a pack is formally chartered there, count toward this measure. A one-time table
does not itself trigger this measure; a chartered den or pack meeting regularly would.

Evidence strength: that a chartered pack counts as an extracurricular activity is
`[district's own document]` by definition of the metric. That participation in it produces
any measurable learning-experience gain beyond the count itself is
`[argument — no direct evidence]`.

## Goal 1 alignment (district-general)

Goal 1 is Student Achievement. Its relevant sub-goals — 1.1 (SEL) and 1.2 (MTSS) — are
supporting alignment here, not the lead argument, because the district's own Theory of Action
places stakeholder engagement causally upstream of achievement, and because the strongest
research behind both sub-goals was generated for a different delivery model than a Cub Scout den,
as the caveats below make clear.

### 1.1 — Social-Emotional Learning

District's own goal statement: _"Develop and implement a comprehensive social-emotional
learning (SEL) framework."_ `[district's own document]`. No SEL curriculum vendor is named
publicly (`notes/strategic-plan.md`), which means there is no existing program to compete
against, but also no district language connecting SEL specifically to any outside youth
program.

The strongest SEL evidence base — Durlak et al. (2011), an 11-percentile-point academic
gain across 213 studies and 270,034 students, and Taylor et al. (2017), a follow-up
meta-analysis with comparable effect sizes — is `[peer-reviewed]`, but both studies evaluate
**school-delivered, curriculum-based SEL programs during the school day**, not out-of-school
youth organizations (`notes/sel.md`). Their effect sizes do not transfer to a Cub Scout den
without a separate argument, and that argument has not been tested.

CASEL's own framework states that SEL happens across classrooms, schools, homes, and
communities `[peer-reviewed-adjacent — organization's stated framework]`
([casel.org](https://casel.org/casel-sel-framework-11-2020/)). Two claims in the underlying
research notes go further than CASEL's own text and must be demoted:

- The claim that this framework makes youth organizations "essential partners" is
  **not CASEL's claim** — it does not appear in CASEL's own language. It is
  `[argument — no direct evidence]`, an inference the notes draw from the four-settings
  model, not a CASEL finding.
- The claim that community programs "reduce district burden" is similarly
  `[argument — no direct evidence]`, not something CASEL states.

Both may still be made in a pitch, but only labeled as the pack's own reasoning, not
attributed to CASEL.

The RAND/Wallace Foundation PSELI study of 100+ afterschool programs found positive gains
in feelings, attitudes, and behavioral adjustment in out-of-school-time settings; this is an
independent research-organization report, not a peer-reviewed journal study, and it is not
Scouting-specific (`notes/sel.md`). The SAFE design principles (Sequenced, Active, Focused,
Explicit) it describes are a reasonable checklist for a pack's own program design, not
evidence that Cub Scouting already meets them.

### 1.2 — Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS)

District's own goal statement: _"Build a multi-tiered system of supports (MTSS) that
equitably meets the academic and behavioral needs of all students."_ `[district's own
document]`. Georgia's version, GaMTSS, explicitly calls for "teamwork" among "educators,
staff, students, families, and community partners" `[district/state document]`
(`notes/mtss.md`, citing gadoe.org).

School connectedness — students feeling that adults and peers care about them — is a
CDC-documented protective factor `[peer-reviewed/federal research synthesis]` linked to
better attendance, grades, and graduation, and reduced risk behaviors
(`notes/mtss.md`, citing CDC). The "one caring adult" and Search Institute's Developmental
Relationships Framework are well-supported in educational and family settings
`[peer-reviewed]`, but the research notes are explicit that **the evidence for
out-of-school-time programs, including youth organizations, serving this role is "growing
but less comprehensive"** — not absent, but not as robust as the school-based evidence
(`notes/mtss.md`).

The notes' own MTSS researcher states this caveat directly: _"the direct empirical evidence
linking specific community programs like Cub Scouts to school MTSS outcomes is limited"_ and
that the theoretical alignment, while sound, "would ideally be supported by formal
evaluation data specific to the program." This must be carried into the pitch verbatim in
spirit: the structural analogy (den as a Tier 1 belonging space, mentoring as a Tier 2-type
function) is `[argument — no direct evidence]`, not a documented MTSS finding.

### Where Cub Scout adventure content overlaps a math-and-science magnet's interests — honestly

Alexander II's own mission statement emphasizes math, science, and technology
(`notes/alexander-ii.md`, citing the school's own site), and the school runs a FIRST LEGO Robotics
Team as an extracurricular activity (`notes/alexander-ii.md`, school-specific fact).

Cub Scouting's advancement structure is organized around six required pillars at every rank —
Character & Leadership, Personal Fitness, Personal Safety, Family & Reverence, Citizenship, and
Outdoors — confirmed directly on Scouting America's current official rank-requirement pages for
Tiger and Webelos (`notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §3, citing
[scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adventures/tiger](https://www.scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adventures/tiger/)
and
[scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adventures/webelos](https://www.scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adventures/webelos/)).
**Cub Scouts earn rank adventures and awards, not merit badges — merit badges belong to Scouts
BSA, the program for older youth, not Cub Scouts.** Any reference to "merit badges" in connection
with Cub Scout-age children is incorrect and must not appear in this pitch.

The honest overlap: Cub Scouting's structure is not a STEM curriculum, and no adventure pillar is
named "STEM." The closest genuine overlaps are:

- The Outdoors and Personal Fitness pillars involve hands-on, observation-based activity at every
  rank, which is directionally consistent with a magnet school's emphasis on hands-on learning, but
  this is a structural similarity, not a shared curriculum — `[argument — no direct evidence]`.
- Individual elective adventures with STEM-adjacent content exist at some ranks (current official
  requirements should be checked rank-by-rank at
  [scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adventures](https://www.scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adventures/)
  before naming any specific one in a pitch, since elective offerings change).
- **This pitch does not rest on, and must not cite, the national STEM Nova or Super Nova Awards.**
  That program is being discontinued as a national offering effective June 1, 2025
  (`notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §3, and the must-not-claim list below). Any STEM-Scouting
  claim needs its current local-council status confirmed before it is used at all.

The honest bottom line for this school: a Cub Scout pack is not a STEM program and should not be
sold as one. What it offers a math-and-science magnet is the same thing it offers any school —
engaged, vetted parent volunteers and family attendance — plus a modest thematic resonance (hands-on
activity, structured small groups) that is worth a sentence, not a slide.

### If a school has no Pack 170 families enrolled

The "our families are already here" framing is specific to Alexander II and does not transfer to a
school where the pack has no existing families. At such a school, the case has to rest entirely on
the district-general Goal 3/Goal 1 alignment above and the risk answers below, without the
insider standing this section describes. What substitutes:

- Lead with the formal channel rather than an existing relationship: the Office of Partnerships
  route or a direct approach to that school's PTO, since there is no informal "our families" opening
  to use instead (see "The practical ask").
- Bring the same unasked documentation described under "What a cautious administrator is
  protecting against" — it substitutes for the trust an existing relationship would otherwise
  provide.
- Do not claim or imply any prior connection to a school where the pack has no enrolled families;
  present the pack plainly as an outside organization there, and expect a slower, more formal
  process as a result. This is the weaker case, and the pitch should not pretend otherwise.

## Opportunity, not risk (district-general)

A district audience will raise these questions whether or not a pack brings them up.
Answering them first is the difference between "opportunity" and "risk."

**Youth protection requirements.** Two adult leaders, both at least 21, are required at
every Scouting activity ("two-deep leadership"); one-on-one adult-youth contact outside a
parent/child relationship is prohibited; Youth Protection Training is mandatory for every
registered adult leader and must be renewed; criminal background checks are required for
every registered adult leader as part of national registration. These are current, national,
mandatory policies, not aspirational guidance, effective in their current form since
September 1, 2023. Source: Scouting America's Guide to Safe Scouting
([scouting.org/health-and-safety/gss/gss01](https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/gss/gss01/)),
via `notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §4.1.

**Abuse history and the settlement trust.** Boy Scouts of America (now Scouting America)
filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy in February 2020, driven by a large volume of historical child
sexual abuse claims. A Plan of Reorganization was confirmed in 2022, funding a Scouting
Settlement Trust at roughly $2.4 billion to compensate survivors — one of the largest
sexual-abuse compensation funds in U.S. history. More than 85% of survivors voting on the
plan supported it; a federal court upheld the plan and the U.S. Supreme Court declined to
review it. The Trust is still actively administering claims — this is not closed history.
The current youth-protection architecture above is the organization's direct, repeatedly
tightened response to this history. Source: Scouting Newsroom press releases, PBS, court
reporting, via `notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §4.2. This should be stated plainly to a
district, not minimized or omitted.

**Insurance.** Scouting America carries a national general liability program covering all
chartered organizations on file — reported at $1M per occurrence / $10M aggregate primary
coverage, with excess layers above that — funded through the national membership fee, not a
separate bill to the chartering organization. A school hosting or chartering a unit is not
itself purchasing or underwriting this insurance. Source:
[scouting.org/health-and-safety/gss/gss10](https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/gss/gss10/),
via `notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §4.3.

**Cost to the school.** A single table at an existing event has no facilities cost beyond the
space the event already uses. The standard chartered-organization relationship, if a pack later
seeks one, typically requires only meeting-space use; there is no rent obligation to BSA and no
separate insurance bill. No source quantifies district-wide facilities cost for a recurring
arrangement (custodial time, after-hours building access) — this is a real, unresolved question
for that heavier scenario, flagged as **not yet answered,** not as zero
(`notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §4.4).

**Membership policy.** Scouting America's stated policy welcomes all eligible youth
"regardless of race, ethnic background, gender or orientation." Girls were admitted to all
programs, Cub Scouts through Eagle Scout, nationwide, effective February 1, 2019. In
early-to-mid 2026, Scouting America reached an agreement with the U.S. Department of
Defense reported to involve framing membership around "biological sex at birth"; Scouting
America's own public statement disputes that this changes its transgender-youth policy, and
a lawsuit/FOIA complaint was filed in mid-2026 to clarify the actual terms. **This is
contested and unresolved** — present it as an open, current-events item, not a settled fact
in either direction. Source: AP, PBS, via `notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §4.5.

**Religious affiliation of chartered organizations.** Scouting has operated through
chartered community organizations — churches, civic clubs, and schools among them — since
its 1910 founding; it does not own its own meeting space. The single largest historical
chartering body was the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, which chartered
Scouting as its young-men's program from 1913 until it ended the affiliation at the close of
2019 — schools were never the dominant charter type. Pack 170's own charter organization
should be stated plainly if asked, since "what kind of unit is this, exactly" is a fair
question; national policy (membership, youth protection, insurance) applies uniformly
regardless of charter type. Source: `notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §2 and §4.6.

**Pack 170's own charter, stated plainly.** Pack 170 is chartered by Highland Hills Baptist
Church, which charters all three Scouting America units in the 170 family
(`docs/Offical-info.md`, the pack's canonical fact file) [district-independent, pack's own
record]. Two facts belong in the same breath, because a public-school administrator will
weigh them together: families need no connection to the chartering church to join, and the
pack's public site already says so in its own words. The church provides the pack's regular
meeting space, which is also why the pack needs nothing from a school building. Do not let
this surface as a discovery mid-conversation; say it first.

## What a cautious administrator is actually protecting against (district-general)

This section is written generically. It does not describe any named person's disposition, and it
should not be edited to attach a personality trait to any principal, at Alexander II or anywhere
else in the district. The premise is structural, not personal: **any administrator with no prior
relationship to the pack has no reason to extend trust yet, and bears a real cost if they have to
defend an unusual decision later** — to a supervisor, to the district, or to another parent who
asks "why them and not us." That premise holds at every BCSD school. The list below is what that
cost is actually made of, and what answers it at no cost to the administrator.

- **Liability and insurance.** Answered directly above: Scouting America's national general
  liability program covers chartered units, funded by the national membership fee, not a bill to
  the school (`notes/scouting-and-education.md`, §4.3). A council certificate of insurance handed
  over unasked removes this as an open question rather than something the administrator has to
  chase down.
- **Precedent and equity — "if we let this group table, do we have to let every group table?"**
  This is a legitimate concern, and the honest answer is that the district already maintains a
  category for it rather than the school having to invent an exception. BCSD's own partner
  framework distinguishes sustained, aligned groups from one-time or promotional visitors
  `[district's own document]` (`notes/strategic-plan.md`, citing
  [bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/whatisapartner](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/whatisapartner)),
  and a PTO that runs its own event-vendor or table policy is applying a rule it already has, not
  making a one-off exception for Scouting. The answer to "why them" is "the same rule that applies
  to anyone else asking for a table," not "because we like this group."
- **Instructional time.** A table at a family event outside instructional hours costs no classroom
  time. This is a smaller claim than the meeting-space ask this pitch explicitly does not make (see
  "The real ask, stated up front"), and it should be said plainly to close the question rather than
  left implicit.
- **Optics of a chartered unit's affiliation in a public school.** Addressed above under religious
  affiliation: the pack's own charter organization should be named without prompting, and national
  policy applies the same way regardless of charter type. An administrator should not have to ask
  who charters the unit; the pitch should say it first.
- **Being asked to personally justify the decision.** This is the cost the rest of this section
  exists to remove. A decision that runs through an existing, named category — a PTO's own
  event-table policy, or the district's own Partner framework — is a decision the administrator can
  point to a rule for, rather than one they made alone. See "The practical ask" for why the formal
  channel is the lighter path here, not the heavier one.

## The practical ask

**What a pack should actually ask for:** a table at one specific, already-scheduled family event —
an open house, a fall festival, a family night — staffed by named adults who are already
background-checked and Youth-Protection-trained. As lighter, secondary asks: a few minutes on a
PTO meeting agenda, or permission to post a flyer. **Not meeting space, not a room, not a
recurring program the school has to run or supervise.** A standing arrangement can be discussed
later, once this smaller ask has already gone well.

### The open procedural question to ask first

BCSD's published policy, **Board Regulation IFCD-R**, requires a criminal background check for
any campus volunteer, but it does not state whether an adult who already holds an approved IFCD-R
background check may staff a table for an outside group at a school-hosted or PTO-run family
event `[district's own document]` (`notes/strategic-plan.md`, `notes/alexander-ii.md`, citing
[heritage.bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteering](https://heritage.bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteering) and
[bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteer](https://www.bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteer)). This is cheap to
resolve: ask the school's front office, or the district Volunteer Office (478-765-8711), directly.
Do not assume the answer either way before asking.

### Who decides

If the target event is run by the PTO rather than the school itself, the decision about who gets
a table may belong to the PTO, not the principal. This research did not find a document that
states, for any specific BCSD school, who owns that decision. **Where the event is PTO-run, ask the
PTO first** — it is very plausibly a smaller door than asking the principal, since it does not
require the principal to personally grant anything.

### Treat the formal channel as cover, not overhead

The heavier-seeming path is often the lighter one for an administrator with no reason yet to
extend trust. A request that arrives already inside an existing, named category — the PTO's own
table policy, or a district Partner listing — reads as "already sanctioned," not as "a decision I
have to personally own." **If a standing partner listing is wanted later**, the formal option is
the **Office of Partnerships, Projects and Engagement** — 478-765-8762, partnerships@bcsdk12.net,
or the
[Partnership Interest Form](https://forms.cloud.microsoft/r/KPtZnA7srz). The district's own
process, quoted: _"Your organization's mission and goals will be reviewed for alignment. If
approved, you'll complete the Partnership Agreement and an onboarding process, which may
include orientation, training, and background checks (if applicable)."_ `[district's own
document]` (`notes/strategic-plan.md`, `notes/district-profile.md`). **Event sponsorship
specifically requires 45 days' notice** via the District Sponsorship Request Form
(`notes/alexander-ii.md`, citing
[bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/district-project-support](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/district-project-support)).
This research did **not** establish that a formal partner agreement is required merely to table at
a PTO-run event — that heavier route is an option if a standing listing is wanted, not a
requirement for the small ask above.

### Arrive with documentation unasked

A pack should hand over the following before being asked for it, since an administrator with no
reason yet to extend trust says yes faster when nothing is left to ask for:

- Youth Protection Training completion confirmation for the specific adults who will staff the
  table.
- Confirmation of current background-check status for those same named adults.
- A certificate of insurance from the local council, showing the national general liability
  coverage described above.
- The pack's own public youth-protection information (whatever page or document the pack maintains
  describing its safety rules).
- The names of any pack adults who are already approved BCSD volunteers under IFCD-R, if
  applicable at that school.

### Make the first ask bounded

The first ask should be a single event: one table, one named date, defined hours, named adults,
nothing recurring. A standing arrangement, a PTO meeting slot on a regular cadence, or a formal
partner listing are all reasonable next steps, but only after the bounded first event has gone
well. Asking for all of it at once reintroduces the precedent and time-commitment worries the
bounded version avoids.

## What we must not claim

The following do not appear anywhere in this pitch, because the research could not support
them:

- **Cub Scouts do not earn merit badges.** Merit badges belong to Scouts BSA, the program for
  older youth. Cub Scouts earn rank adventures and awards. Do not use "merit badge" in connection
  with Cub Scout-age children anywhere in this pitch or the resulting web page.
- **An existing BCSD volunteer approval (IFCD-R) covers staffing a table for an outside group at a
  school event.** This is unverified — see "The practical ask." Do not assume or imply that
  already-approved status answers this question until the school or district confirms it.
- **The school or district endorses Scouting.** No document reviewed in this research says so. A
  school agreeing to a table, a flyer, or a PTO meeting slot is not an endorsement, and the pitch
  must not describe it as one.
- **No study of Scouting uses independent school records** — grades, attendance,
  disciplinary data — as an outcome measure for Cub-Scout-age children. Every character/
  outcome claim in the Scouting literature rests on self-report, not school records.
- **The "73% more likely to vote" Eagle Scout statistic could not be traced to a primary
  source.** It appears only on secondary/alumni pages. Do not cite it.
- **The Merrill Lynch/Age Wave "Value of Scouting" figures could not be located or
  verified in this research pass.** Do not cite a number from this source.
- **The national STEM Nova and Super Nova Awards program is being discontinued effective
  June 1, 2025.** No STEM pitch may rest on this program being an active national offering;
  confirm current local-council status before mentioning STEM Scouting at all.
- **Baylor Institute for Studies of Religion findings and the Harris Poll "Scouting Edge"
  and 2026 Eagle Scout studies are organization-commissioned self-report research,** funded
  or co-sponsored by Scouting America, and should be presented as such — not as independent
  academic replication.
- **Schools were never the dominant chartering organization historically.** The Church of
  Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints was the largest single chartering body until 2019. Do
  not imply that school-based charters are, or have ever been, Scouting's primary model.
- **CASEL does not describe youth organizations as "essential partners"** and does not
  state that community programs "reduce district burden." Both are inferences a research
  note drew from CASEL's four-settings framework, not statements CASEL itself makes. Present
  them only as the pack's own argument.
- **"Cub Scouting directly aligns with district needs" is the pitch's conclusion, not a
  finding to state upfront.** It has to be earned through the sub-goal-by-sub-goal case
  above, not asserted as an established fact at the outset.
- **No current-year (post-2022-23) BCSD Goal 3 progress data was located.** Do not claim
  the district has already made or lost ground on any Goal 3 metric since the baseline was
  set.
- **No BCSD document connects Scouting or Cub Scouts specifically to any goal in the
  strategic plan.** Do not imply prior district endorsement.
- **No SEL curriculum vendor is named in BCSD's plan.** Do not claim a pack is filling a
  slot the district has already defined.
- **No study measures whether Scout-parent volunteering predicts subsequent school
  volunteering.** The overlap between BSA's vetting requirement and school volunteer
  requirements is a structural argument, not a measured causal finding.
- **No study measures the effect of a chartered youth organization operating inside a
  school building** on achievement or stakeholder-engagement metrics, as distinct from
  general community-school partnership models (health clinics, food pantries, tutoring).
  Rely on the general family-engagement research instead of implying a Scouting-specific
  study exists.
- **The practical, legal effect of the 2026 Pentagon/DoD membership agreement is actively
  disputed.** Do not characterize it as settled in either direction.
- **The exact district-wide facilities cost of hosting a recurring unit (custodial time,
  after-hours access) has not been quantified** by any source found. Do not promise it is free for
  that heavier scenario; say it is typically limited to space use and ask the school directly.
- **Do not characterize any named district or school employee's personality or disposition** —
  cautious, distant, guarded, welcoming, or otherwise — anywhere in this pitch or on the resulting
  web page. Any strategy premise about how an administrator might respond should be stated
  generically (see "What a cautious administrator is actually protecting against"), never
  attributed to a specific named individual.
