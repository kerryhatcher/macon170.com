# BCSD Research — Index

Compiled: 2026-07-27.

This research supports a pitch from Cub Scout Pack 170 (Macon, Georgia) to Bibb County
School District (BCSD): principals, PTA/PTO boards, and the district's Office of
Partnerships. The goal is to get BCSD to see a Cub Scout pack as stakeholder-engagement
infrastructure the district's own strategic plan already asks for, not as an outside
program asking for a favor.

## What's here

- **`SYNTHESIS.md`** — the main document. Audience fears, the Goal 3 opening, sub-goal by
  sub-goal alignment to Goal 3 and Goal 1, the risk register, the practical ask, what we
  must not claim, and open questions.
- **`talking-points.md`** — a one-pager a volunteer can carry into a meeting: the argument
  in plain language, a handful of sourced facts, and answers to the objections a principal
  or partnerships officer will actually raise.
- **`notes/`** — the five researchers' raw source notes this synthesis draws from:
  - `district-profile.md` — BCSD size, demographics, leadership, partnership infrastructure
  - `strategic-plan.md` — the 2023–2028 #Built4Bibb plan, Goals 1–3, Theory of Action, and
    Goal 3's own baselines and targets
  - `sel.md` — the CASEL framework and the social-emotional-learning evidence base
  - `mtss.md` — Georgia's Multi-Tiered System of Supports, tiers, school connectedness,
    attendance
  - `scouting-and-education.md` — Scouting outcome research, history with schools, program
    content, risk history, and family-engagement evidence

## Bottom line

BCSD's own Theory of Action names stakeholder engagement as the causal input that drives
staff effectiveness and student achievement, and the district's own baseline data shows a
62-point gap on its single biggest engagement metric (student "Show Up" commitment, 33%
against a 95% target); a volunteer-run Cub Scout pack is a concrete, countable instance of
the exact thing Goal 3 asks the district to build more of — a family that shows up, an
adult who is trained and background-checked, and a standing relationship between families
and a school building — and the supporting SEL and MTSS research, plus a candid answer to
every risk question a principal will raise, exists to back that argument up, not replace
it.
