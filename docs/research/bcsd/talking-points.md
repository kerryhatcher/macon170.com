# Talking Points: Pack 170 and Bibb County Schools

A one-pager for a volunteer walking into a meeting with a principal, a PTA/PTO board, or
the Office of Partnerships. Say these out loud. Don't read them off a slide.

Compiled 2026-07-27. Full sourcing in `SYNTHESIS.md`.

---

## The one-sentence argument

"Your district's own strategic plan says the way you raise achievement is by building
stakeholder engagement first — more families showing up, more trained adults involved,
stronger trust between the school and the home. A Cub Scout pack is exactly that: a group
of families who show up every month, with an adult from each family who's been trained and
background-checked, meeting at your school. We're not asking you to add a new program. We
fit a category you already track."

## Four facts to have ready

1. **BCSD's own plan says engagement comes first.** The district's Theory of Action states:
   *"if we build stakeholder engagement... and strengthen staff effectiveness... we will
   maximize student achievement."* That's their language, not ours.
   (bcsdk12.net/about-us/strategic-plan)

2. **The district's own numbers show the gap.** Only 33% of students are "committed to
   Show Up," against a 95% target the district itself set. That's the district's biggest
   self-reported gap in the whole plan. (BCSD Specific Performance Measures document,
   2022–23 baseline)

3. **The district counts partners like us by name.** BCSD tracks "# of business/community
   partners actively engaged" — 337 today, aiming for 1,000. A chartered pack registered
   through the Office of Partnerships is one of those, on the district's own scoreboard.

4. **Every den leader is background-checked and trained, same as any school volunteer.**
   Scouting requires two trained adults at every meeting, a criminal background check for
   every registered leader, and no one-on-one contact between an adult and a child who
   isn't their own. That's not a promise — it's a national, mandatory rule.
   (scouting.org, Guide to Safe Scouting)

## The objections, and the honest answer

**"Isn't there a Boy Scouts abuse scandal?"**
Yes, and don't dodge it. Scouting America went through Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2020
because of a huge number of historical abuse claims. They settled by putting $2.4 billion
into a victims' trust — more than 85% of survivors who voted on it supported the plan. The
background-check and two-adult rules we just described exist *because of* that history, not
in spite of it. It happened, it was bad, and the response is why the rules are as strict as
they are today.

**"What does this cost the school?"**
Nothing beyond letting us use a room once a month. Scouting's national insurance covers
every chartered unit — the school doesn't buy separate coverage. We don't have a hard
number on custodial or after-hours-access cost, so ask us for that, and we'll ask the
council. We're not going to pretend it's zero if it isn't.

**"Is this a religious program?"**
No. Lots of Scout units are chartered by churches, historically more than by schools, but
the national rules on safety, insurance, and membership apply the same way no matter who
charters the unit. A pack at your school would be its own thing, not tied to any church's
version of Scouting.

**"Are girls allowed? What about our nondiscrimination policy?"**
Girls have been eligible for every Scouting program, Cub Scouts through Eagle, since 2019.
The national policy welcomes youth regardless of race, background, gender, or orientation.
There's a live, contested dispute right now about a 2026 agreement with the Department of
Defense over how membership is described — we don't know how that shakes out yet, and we
won't pretend we do.

**"Doesn't this compete with the PTA or the Mentor's Project?"**
We're not asking to replace anything. We want one thing: a room, once a month, and to be
listed as a partner the way the district already lists partners. If a family is already
stretched thin, that's not new — we're one more option, not a competing demand.

## What to actually ask for

Don't ask for a district-wide deal. Ask one school's principal for two things:

1. **Meeting space** — cafeteria, gym, or media center, once a month, after school hours.
2. **To be listed as a partner** through the Office of Partnerships
   (478-765-8762, partnerships@bcsdk12.net) — the district already has a tier for exactly
   this: monthly volunteers, hosting school events.

That's the ask. Everything else in this document is backup for when someone pushes back.
