# Talking Points: Pack 170 and Bibb County Schools

A one-pager for a pack adult talking to another parent, a PTO board, or a principal's front
office. Say these out loud. Don't read them off a slide. This is a conversation between people
who already share a school, not a pitch from an outside group.

Compiled 2026-07-27. Revised 2026-07-27 for the corrected ask. Full sourcing in `SYNTHESIS.md`.

---

## The one-sentence version

"Our families are already here. A lot of us already volunteer in the building. We'd like a table
at the open house, same as anybody else who tables at a family event, so families who want this
for their kids know it exists."

## What to actually ask for

Not a room. Not a standing program. Just:

1. **A table at one specific event** — the open house, the fall festival, whatever's already on
   the calendar — staffed by named adults who are already background-checked and trained.
2. As a lighter, second option: a few minutes on a PTO meeting agenda, or permission to post a
   flyer.

That's it for the first ask. If it goes well, a standing arrangement can be a later
conversation. Don't ask for everything at once.

## If it's a PTO event, ask the PTO first

If the open house or family night is run by the PTO, the PTO, not the principal, may be the one
who decides who gets a table. That's a smaller, easier door. Try it first.

## Come with the paperwork before anyone asks for it

Bring, unprompted:

- Youth Protection Training confirmation for whoever's staffing the table.
- Confirmation those same adults have a current background check.
- A certificate of insurance from the council.
- Whatever page or handout the pack has on its own youth-protection rules.
- The names of any pack adults who are already approved BCSD volunteers at that school.

Handing this over before being asked answers the questions before they're raised.

## Four facts to have ready

1. **BCSD's own plan says engagement comes first.** The district's Theory of Action states:
   *"if we build stakeholder engagement... and strengthen staff effectiveness... we will
   maximize student achievement."* That's their language.
   (bcsdk12.net/about-us/strategic-plan)

2. **The district's own numbers show the gap.** Only 33% of students are "committed to
   Show Up," against a 95% target the district itself set. That's the district's biggest
   self-reported gap in the whole plan. (BCSD Specific Performance Measures document,
   2022–23 baseline)

3. **Many of us are already approved volunteers.** Two adults are required at every den meeting,
   and both must pass a criminal background check and Youth Protection Training. Several of our
   families already hold BCSD's own volunteer approval for other reasons. We're not new faces
   asking to be let in.

4. **Cub Scouts earn adventures, not merit badges.** Merit badges are a Scouts BSA thing, for
   older kids. Don't say "merit badge" when you mean Cub Scouts, it's a factual error and people
   who know Scouting will notice.

## The objections, and the honest answer

**"Isn't there a Boy Scouts abuse scandal?"**
Yes, and don't dodge it. Scouting America went through Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2020 because of a
huge number of historical abuse claims. They settled by putting $2.4 billion into a victims'
trust, and more than 85% of survivors who voted on it supported the plan. The background-check
and two-adult rules exist *because of* that history, not in spite of it.

**"What does this cost the school?"**
Nothing. It's a table at an event you're already running, for a few hours, staffed by people
who've already cleared your own volunteer process. There's no room to give up and no insurance
bill, Scouting's national coverage handles that.

**"If we let you table, don't we have to let every group table?"**
That's a fair question, and the honest answer is you probably already have a rule for this,
whatever your event's usual policy is for outside tables or vendors. We're not asking for an
exception. We're asking to be treated like anyone else who follows that same rule.

**"Is this a religious program?"**
Say it before you're asked. Pack 170 is chartered by Highland Hills Baptist Church, which is
where we meet. No family needs any connection to that church to join, and our website says so
in plain sight. Most Scout units nationally are chartered by a church, a civic club, or a
similar community group, and the safety, insurance, and membership rules are identical no
matter who charters the unit. We also aren't asking to move our meetings into the school, so
nothing about the church comes with us to an open house table.

**"Are girls allowed?"**
Yes, since 2019, for every Scouting program from Cub Scouts through Eagle. There's a live,
unsettled dispute right now about a 2026 agreement with the Department of Defense over how
membership is described. We don't know how it resolves, and we won't pretend we do.

**"Doesn't this compete with the PTA or other volunteer commitments?"**
We're not asking to replace anything or take a room. A table for a few hours is not a competing
demand on anyone's time.

## What we're not asking for, on purpose

Meeting space. A key. A recurring slot the school has to manage or supervise. If a standing
relationship makes sense later, that's a separate, later conversation, likely through the
district's Office of Partnerships (478-765-8762, partnerships@bcsdk12.net). The table is the ask.
Everything else in `SYNTHESIS.md` is backup for when someone pushes back.
