# Bibb County School District (BCSD) 2023–2028 Strategic Plan — Research Notes

Scope: BCSD (Macon, Georgia — `bcsdk12.net`). Do not confuse with the separate "Bibb County Schools" district in Alabama (`bibbed.org`), whose strategic plan surfaced in search results but is a different district with a different Goal 3 ("safe learning environment") — excluded from this research.

Research date: 2026-07-27.

## Branding — verified against the given structure

Confirmed via the district's own strategic-plan page and its FY2024 Annual Comprehensive Financial Report (ACFR):

- **#Built4Bibb** (also written `B4B`) is the correct hashtag/brand for the plan.
- **"More Victory Planned"** is confirmed — the strategy-map graphic file is literally named `B4BMVPStrategyMap.jpg` ("B4B MVP"), and a 2024 Leadership Symposium was titled "More Victory Planned: Together 4 School Improvement." (Source: [bcsdk12.net/about-us/strategic-plan](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/strategic-plan); Facebook, bibbschools, 2024.)
- **4ME** is confirmed but the given prompt's structure did not include its expansion. The district's FY2024 ACFR spells it out as the **"4 M.E. Framework"** of Learner Outcomes: *"Matched and Enrolled [to a secondary learning institution], Motivated to Enlist [in the armed forces], Molded for Entrepreneurship, [and] Meaningfully Employed [in the workforce]."* (Source: Bibb County School District FY2024 ACFR, p. vi, hosted at ted.cviog.uga.edu.) The accreditation page separately confirms "#B4B strategic plan and its focus on the 4ME outcomes." (Source: [bcsdk12.net/about-us/accreditation](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/accreditation).)
- The pillar/sub-goal structure given in the prompt **matches the district's own strategic-plan page exactly**, including numbering (1.1–1.4, 2.1–2.5, 3.1–3.3) and sub-goal titles. One correction: the district's page and ACFR consistently label the pillars **"Goal 1/2/3,"** not "Pillar 1/2/3" — I've kept "Goal" going forward to match district usage.
- The **Theory of Action** quote given in the prompt is confirmed nearly verbatim. The district's own wording, quoted from the strategic-plan page: *"The Bibb County School District's Theory of Action is that if we build stakeholder engagement of students, parents staff and community, and strengthen staff effectiveness to drive teaching and learning, we will maximize student achievement."* (Source: [bcsdk12.net/about-us/strategic-plan](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/strategic-plan).) This directly and explicitly frames stakeholder engagement as **causally prior to** (an input to) student achievement — not a parallel, equal-weight goal. The FY2024 ACFR restates this design intent: *"Three key areas will guide the District's work under the Strategic Plan – student achievement, staff effectiveness and stakeholder engagement."* (Source: BCSD FY2024 ACFR, p. vi.)

Plan approval: *"The Bibb County Board of Education approved the Bibb County School District's 2023-2028 Strategic Plan, #Built4Bibb: More Victory Planned, on June 15, 2023."* (Source: BCSD FY2024 ACFR, and Board Brief newsletter dated June 15, 2023 — [bb_061523.pdf](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1710389170/bibbk12gaus/am5yud7zj1htnn0bsdhb/bb_061523.pdf), headline "#Built4Bibb Strategic Plan Approved.")

District mission/vision (ACFR, p. vi):
- Vision (as restated in ACFR intro): *"maximizes student achievement and social-emotional well-being by building a sense of community in safe, equitable, learning environments."*
- Learner Profile: *"Boldly Adapt," "Communicate & Collaborate," "Solve Problems," "Demonstrate Empathy."*
- Guiding Principles: *"Personalized Learning," "Collaboration," "Engagement," "Safety," "Effective Leadership."*

## Goal 3: Stakeholder Engagement (primary focus — deepest research below)

District's own goal statement, quoted exactly: *"We will build collective ownership and active engagement among all stakeholders to maximize students' overall learning experience."* (Source: BCSD "Specific Performance Measures" PDF; near-identically in ACFR: *"We will build collective ownership and active engagement amongst ALL stakeholders to maximize students' overall learning experiences."*)

This is the goal the Theory of Action names as the starting input: engagement is framed as what the district *builds* first, in order to *strengthen* staff effectiveness, in order to *maximize* student achievement. The causal chain is explicit in the district's own language, not an inference — see the Theory of Action quote above.

### The district's own admitted gap

The single most usable fact for a partner pitch: **the district's own published baseline shows only 33% of students "committed to Show Up," against a target of 95%** — a 62-point self-identified gap, the largest spread of any Goal 3 metric with a real baseline. Staff already sit near-target (87% → 95%), but student and parent buy-in are the lagging indicators. Most other Goal 3 measures — parent volunteering, parent meeting attendance, Title I survey response, Parent Portal registration, extracurricular participation, student-led conference attendance, business partner event attendance — were listed as **"TBD"** at the 2022–23 baseline, meaning the district had not yet measured them when the plan was adopted; I could not find a later public data point showing where these landed. This is a gap in what I could verify, not a claim about actual performance — flagged explicitly as "not found" below.

The two engagement-index measures did have real 2022–23 baselines that show room to grow: student engagement index 3.83 and staff engagement index 4.04, both against a 4.95 target (scale not specified in the source; presumably a 5-point survey scale, but the document does not state this explicitly — do not present the scale as confirmed).

### Family/partner/volunteer infrastructure that operationalizes Goal 3

**"Partner" definition and process (Office of Partnerships, Projects & Engagement):**
- The district's own definition: *"A Strategic Partner is an individual, business, nonprofit, or organization that: Supports the district's Strategic Plan / Provides sustained, aligned support for students and staff / Committed to enhance students learning experiences to support impactful academic outcomes."* (Source: [bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/whatisapartner](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/whatisapartner).)
- What is explicitly **excluded**: *"One-time donors without continued engagement,"* *"Vendors seeking only transactional relationships,"* *"Organizations or groups seeking promotional access to students,"* *"Visitors who attend without meaningful interaction."* This matters for how a Cub Scout pack should frame itself — as sustained programming, not a one-time event or promotional presence.
- Named partnership types: **School Supporters, District and School Level Partners** (the example given is *"The PUSH to Clearing the Paths"* initiative), **Service Volunteers, Resource Providers, Event and Celebratory Supporters.**
- **"PARTNER UP" Tiered Support/Sponsorship Model** — three tiers by dollar/in-kind contribution: Tier I "Silver" (up to $999: school supplies, small gifts/services), Tier II "Gold" ($1,000–$5,000: monthly volunteers, hosting school events), Tier III "Platinum" ($5,000+: internships, mentoring, multi-year funding). (Source: [bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/whatisapartner](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/whatisapartner) and [.../becoming-a-partner](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/becoming-a-partner), both identical tables.)
- **The actual process to become a partner**, quoted: *"Start by contacting the district via email, phone, or by submitting Partnership Interest Form... Your organization's mission and goals will be reviewed for alignment. If approved, you'll complete the Partnership Agreement and an onboarding process, which may include orientation, training, and background checks (if applicable)."* Contact: Office of Partnerships, 478-765-8762, partnerships@bcsdk12.net. (Source: [bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/becoming-a-partner](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/partnerships/becoming-a-partner).)

**Volunteer process (separate from the partnership track, and mandatory for any in-school presence):**
- District-wide requirement, stated at the school level: *"Background Checks are REQUIRED! All volunteers in the Bibb County School District must submit and have an approved background check on file before you can volunteer. This includes, helping in the classroom, attending field trips, working at Field Day, Book Fair or Holiday Shop, proctoring for testing and any other volunteer job that occurs on campus."* (Source: [heritage.bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteering](https://heritage.bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteering), which points to the district-wide volunteer page and requires a current picture ID + SSN for the background-check application.) The district's central volunteer page ([bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteer](https://www.bcsdk12.net/parents/volunteer)) references **Board Regulation IFCD-R "School Volunteers"** in Simbli (the district's policy portal) as the governing policy, plus a required GaDOE child-abuse-prevention training presentation.

**Business Education Partnership (BEP)** — the district's flagship existing external-partner arrangement, worth understanding as a model/comparison, not something Scouting would join directly:
- *"The Business Education Partnership (BEP) is a collaborative initiative between the Bibb County School District and One Macon."* Core Purpose: *"Support the schools in Bibb County to / Educate all students to / Maximize their potential to / Enable and empower them to / Make meaningful, positive contributions to their communities."* Constituents named: *"Students, Teachers, Administrators, Parents, School Board, Employers, Taxpayers -> COMMUNITY."* (Source: [bcsdk12.net/about-us/business-education-partnership](https://www.bcsdk12.net/about-us/business-education-partnership).)
- One Macon's own scorecard (dated 2019, covering its 2013–2018 community plan, so it predates and is **not** a Goal 3 progress report against the current 2023–2028 B4B plan — flagged as older context, not current) records that the BEP's earlier "Leader in Me" character-education rollout reached 21+ elementary/middle/high schools and was associated with a 41.1% drop in discipline referrals at one pilot school. Included as background on the district's appetite for character/leadership programming delivered through an external partner, not as evidence about the current plan. (Source: [onemacon.org/score-card](https://onemacon.org/score-card/).)

**Family Engagement Program:**
- Mission, quoted: *"The BCSD Family Engagement Program will provide authentic opportunities for families and the community to become full partners in the academic success of every student."* (Source: [bcsdk12.net/parents/family-engagement-program](https://www.bcsdk12.net/parents/family-engagement-program).)
- Names itself responsible for *"the development of programs and strategies to support district-wide engagement of families and community stakeholders,"* and describes *"building dual capacity of both parent and staff knowledge and skills."*
- A 2022–23 Title I Annual Parent Meeting presentation template (school-level, predates full B4B rollout in its "Strategic Priorities" slide but shares the #Built4Bibb tag) lists concrete family-engagement/input channels a Scout pack partnership would sit alongside: *"School Council, PTA/PTO, Title I Survey (November-December), Title I Plans, School-Parent Compact, Parent-Teacher Conferences, Student Led Conferences, District and School Forums, Victory in Schools Parent University (VISPU)"* and volunteer roles: *"Field Trips, Proctor, Office Support, Room Parent, Booster Clubs, Clubs/Organizations, Carpool Duty, Media Center, Cafeteria Duty."* (Source: [FY23 Annual Title I Parent Meeting Presentation](https://cdnsm5-ss19.sharpschool.com/UserFiles/Servers/Server_1742101/File/FY23%20Annual%20Title%20I%20Parent%20Meeting%20Presentation830%20(1).pdf), Veterans Elementary School, dated August 30, 2022 — note this is a school-level template from the 2022–23 school year, one year before B4B Board approval, so treat its "Strategic Priorities" content as transitional/superseded; only the Title I mechanics and engagement-channel list are cited here.)

### Board presentations / progress reports against Goal 3 — what I could and couldn't find

I could not locate a public board presentation, annual progress report, or scorecard that reports **actual current-year results** against the Goal 3 targets above (e.g., an updated "% students committed to Show Up" or "% parents volunteering" figure post-baseline). The only quantified Goal 3 data I could verify is the 2022–23 baseline table in the "Specific Performance Measures" PDF itself, which is explicitly a baseline/target-setting document, not a progress report. District news releases I found (2025) cover graduation rate and CCRPI/Georgia Milestones gains — Goal 1 territory — not Goal 3. **This is a confirmed gap in what's publicly available, not a claim that no such report exists** — I searched board-meeting PowerPoint/PDF patterns, the district's news-releases page, and general web search without finding one.

## Where an outside youth program could plausibly contribute (evidence-based only)

Each item below is tied to a specific district-published sub-goal, measure, or stated program need above — no speculation beyond what the sources support.

- **3.1 Collective Ownership** — the district explicitly counts *"# of business and community stakeholders/partners actively engaged in school and district-related activities"* (baseline 337, target 1,000) and *"% of business and community stakeholders/partners attending district events."* A Cub Scout pack formalized as a district "Partner" (per the district's own partner-vs.-not-a-partner definition, which explicitly wants *sustained, aligned* engagement, not one-time visits) is a countable unit against this exact metric.
- **3.1 "Show Up"** — the district's largest self-admitted gap is student Show-Up commitment (33% baseline vs. 95% target). Any program that increases student and family attendance/engagement rhythm (e.g., a den meeting schedule tied to school attendance encouragement) speaks to a metric the district itself flags as furthest from target — though no source ties Scouting specifically to this metric; this is an inference from the gap, not a district claim.
- **3.2 Mutual Trust & Relationship-Building** — measures include *"% of parents/guardians volunteering"* and *"% of parents/guardians attending academic school meetings per year,"* both TBD at baseline. A pack that recruits parent volunteers through the school and reports those volunteer hours through the district's required background-check/volunteer process would generate exactly the kind of countable parent-volunteering activity this sub-goal measures.
- **3.3 Maximizing Student Learning** — measures include *"% of students participating in extra-curricular activities"* (TBD baseline, 90% target) and *"# of established internships with business/community partners per school high school"* (TBD, 100 target — the internship metric is worded for high schools and is a weaker fit for a Cub Scout pack, which serves K-5). A den or pack chartered at a Bibb elementary school is, on its face, an extracurricular activity countable toward the first of these.
- **1.2 MTSS / PBIS** — the district ties Tier-2/3 behavioral support explicitly to PBIS designations (*"# of schools with PBIS designations of operational or distinguished," 18 → 35*). Scouting's structured, positive-behavior-reinforcing den/pack model is broadly consistent with a PBIS-type framework, but **no district source claims Scouting is or should be part of its PBIS implementation** — this is a structural analogy a pitch could draw, not a documented district position.
- **1.1 SEL** — the district commits to *"a comprehensive social-emotional learning (SEL) framework"* but names no specific curriculum or partner publicly. This is an open door (no named vendor to compete against) but also means there is no district language to cite connecting SEL specifically to any outside youth program — flag any such claim in a pitch as the pack's own argument, not a district endorsement.
- **Partnership Tier structure** — a pack could realistically enter at Tier II ("Gold": *"Monthly volunteers, hosting school events," $1,000–$5,000 equivalent*) given a den's typical cadence of monthly meetings and event participation, per the district's own PARTNER UP tier table.

**Explicitly not supported by any source found:** any claim that BCSD has approved, budgeted for, or previously partnered with Scouting America/Cub Scouts specifically; any current-year (post-baseline) Goal 3 performance data; any named SEL curriculum vendor; any statement from the district connecting stakeholder engagement gains to academic outcomes beyond the Theory of Action's general causal claim.
