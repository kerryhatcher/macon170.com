## Executive Summary — What Pack 170 CAN Do / MUST NOT Do / GRAY AREAS

### ✅ CAN DO

1. **Call itself "Cub Scout Pack 170"** and identify as part of Scouting America and the Central Georgia Council. The charter is the authorization. **[DEFINITIVE]**
2. **Display the official Cub Scouts trademark** (wolf + "Cub Scouts" + fleur-de-lis) on the pack website, flyers, and social media — *unmodified*, in one of the five approved reproductions, with the ® symbol. **[STRONG]**
3. **Use the fleur-de-lis as a digital icon** — the national Brand Guidelines affirmatively recommend it: *"use approved Boy Scouts of America icons (e.g., the fleur-de-lis as an app icon)."* **[DEFINITIVE]**
4. **Use Cub Scout Blue `#003F87` and Cub Scout Gold `#FCD116`** as the site palette. These exact hex values are printed in the official 2024 Scouting America Brand Guidelines. **[DEFINITIVE]**
5. **Download official artwork and customizable templates from the Brand Center** — it exists specifically to serve units, offering materials to *"search, download, and customize … for your pack, troop, or post."* **[STRONG]**
6. **Order custom Pack 170 T-shirts, banners, and patches** — provided they come from a **Scouting America licensed vendor** (ClassB, Custom Ink, and ~100 others). **[DEFINITIVE]**
7. **Run a public pack website and social media presence.** National policy explicitly anticipates unit web presence and gives it rules rather than prohibiting it. **[DEFINITIVE]**

### ❌ MUST NOT DO

1. **Do not alter the Cub Scouts trademark** — no recoloring, retypesetting, tinting/screening, drop shadows, bevels, or glows. **[DEFINITIVE]**
2. **Do not extract the Wolf head and use it as a standalone Pack 170 logo.** The guidelines say verbatim: *"Do not use the Wolf element outside of the approved trademark."* **[DEFINITIVE]**
3. **Do not use the World Scout Emblem (purple WOSM fleur-de-lis)** on pack materials or merchandise. Non-commercial use on products requires a license from the World Scout Bureau, and custom patches depicting the World Scouting Crest are flatly prohibited. **[DEFINITIVE]**
4. **Do not have T-shirts, patches, pins, or any product bearing Scouting marks made by a non-licensed printer** — including a friendly local screen-printer or a parent's Cricut. This is the single most-violated rule in Scouting. **[DEFINITIVE]**
5. **Do not manufacture anything resembling rank insignia, merit badges, patrol emblems, or badges of office.** **[DEFINITIVE]**
6. **Do not publish a Scout's full name.** Official rule: *"When identifying youth members on your website, use first name and last initial only."* **[DEFINITIVE]**
7. **Do not run advertisements, commercial endorsements, or sell merchandise on the site.** **[STRONG]**
8. **Do not imply the pack site is an official Scouting America or council channel.** **[STRONG]**

### ⚠️ GRAY AREAS

1. **Displaying rank badge images (Lion, Tiger, Wolf, Bear, Webelos, Arrow of Light) on the website.** Manufacturing rank insignia is definitively banned; *displaying* official rank artwork in a communication is nowhere prohibited and is near-universal practice. **[INFERRED]** — see [Websites](#5-websites-the-specific-answer).
2. **A custom "Pack 170" logo combining the fleur-de-lis with pack numerals.** No official unit-lockup builder exists. Combining marks is a modification; modifications require Licensing Team permission. **[INFERRED — the safe path is a lockup that places official art *adjacent to*, not merged with, pack identity.]**
3. **Whether a chartered unit counts as a "third party"** for the branded-products rule. The rule's obligation lands on the *manufacturer*, so the practical answer is the same either way. See [Merchandise](#6-merchandise--print-t-shirts-banners-flyers).
4. **Whether the Chartered Organization Representative must approve pack branding.** No official document assigns the COR this duty, but the chartered organization *owns* the unit. **[INFERRED]**
5. **Using AI-generated images of Scouts.** No national rule found; at least one council bans it outright. **[INFERRED]**

## 1. The Legal Foundation — Federal Statute

### 1.1 The 1916 Congressional Charter

Scouting America (chartered as the Boy Scouts of America) holds one of the strongest trademark positions of any organization in the United States, because Congress granted it a statutory monopoly on its marks that operates *in addition to* ordinary Lanham Act trademark law.

The operative provision is **36 U.S.C. § 30905**, titled *"Exclusive right to emblems, badges, marks, and words."* The full statutory text is one sentence plus a savings clause:

> **"The corporation has the exclusive right to use emblems, badges, descriptive or designating marks, and words or phrases the corporation adopts. This section does not affect any vested rights."**

— [36 U.S.C. § 30905 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/36/30905); [Office of the Law Revision Counsel, 36 USC Ch. 309](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=%2Fprelim%40title36%2Fsubtitle2%2FpartB%2Fchapter309&edition=prelim) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

Chapter 309 in full runs § 30901 (Organization) through § 30908 (Annual report). Section 30905 is the only trademark provision; there is no separate criminal-penalty section. The current codification dates to Pub. L. 105–225 (Aug. 12, 1998, 112 Stat. 1326), which recodified without substantive change the original 1916 charter. **[DEFINITIVE]**

> **Citation precision note:** Scouting America's own brand guidelines — both the legacy BSA edition and the 2024 Scouting America edition — cite the charter as *"a 1916 act of Congress (36 U.S.C. 27)."* That is the **pre-1998 codification**. Anyone citing this today should use **36 U.S.C. § 30905**. The official documents are simply out of date on the cite, not wrong on the substance. **[DEFINITIVE]**

### 1.2 Why This Matters More Than Ordinary Trademark Law

Under ordinary trademark law, a plaintiff must prove **likelihood of confusion** to win an infringement claim. Under the congressional charter, Scouting America does not have to.

In ***Wrenn v. Boy Scouts of America***, 2008 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 91913 (N.D. Cal. 2008), the court held that **"BSA need not demonstrate the likelihood of confusion because it has been granted special protection by Congressional charter."** — [Techdirt analysis](https://www.techdirt.com/2013/08/21/congress-gave-boy-scouts-special-law-to-let-them-be-obnoxious-trademark-bullies/) **[STRONG — official secondary; the case itself is unpublished]**

Roughly 115 Title 36 federally chartered organizations hold comparable rights (the American Legion, the U.S. Olympic Committee, and others).

### 1.3 Enforcement Posture — Is This Theoretical?

No. Scouting America actively litigates:

- **1960** — BSA sued "Safety Scouts" over the word *Scouts*, and won.
- **2018–2022** — Girl Scouts of the USA sued BSA over the "Scouts BSA" name; the U.S. District Court granted **summary judgment for BSA**. ([Scouting Newsroom press release](https://www.scoutingnewsroom.org/press-releases/united-states-district-court-rules-in-favor-of-boy-scouts-of-america-grants-bsa-motion-for-summary-judgment-in-trademark-infringement-case-brought-by-gsusa/), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2018/11/07/665308785/girl-scouts-of-the-usa-))
- **February 2026 — ongoing** — Scouting America filed suit in the **Northern District of Texas** against **Queer Scout**, a small Arizona LGBTQ+ travel company, alleging trademark infringement and dilution over the word "Scout." ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/06/03/lgbtq-travel-company-queer-scout-says-scouting-america-sued-over-trademarks-amid-political-pressure/), [KJZZ](https://www.kjzz.org/the-show/2026-05-11/scouting-america-is-suing-this-small-arizona-travel-company-over-its-name-queer-scout), [Metro Weekly](https://www.metroweekly.com/2026/03/scouting-america-sues-queer-scout/))

**[STRONG]**

**The practical read for Pack 170:** every enforcement action found targets **outsiders** — commercial entities, competing youth organizations, unlicensed manufacturers. **No case was found of Scouting America suing one of its own chartered units** for using Scouting marks to run its Scouting program. The charter *is* the license. The risk to a pack is not litigation; it is being told by the council or National to take something down, or having a merchandise order rejected. **[INFERRED]**

### 1.4 The Contractual Hook — Why Brand Guidelines Bind Pack 170

The pack is bound to national brand rules not by trademark law alone, but **by contract**. The **Annual Unit Charter Agreement**, signed every year by the chartered organization and the unit, states at **§III.B.1** that the Scouting Unit agrees to:

> *"Conduct the Scouting program consistent with BSA Bylaws, Rules & Regulations, handbooks, policies, **brand guidelines**, etc."*

— [Annual Charter Agreement (council-hosted copy, Michigan Crossroads Council)](https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Fillable-Annual-Charter-Agreement-Charter-Orgs_Short-Version_09.16.2022.pdf) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source; the scouting.org-hosted copy blocks automated fetching]**

The same agreement, at **§II.A.3**, obligates the **chartered organization** to:

> *"Refrain from using the Scouting brand as a means to imply BSA's endorsement of the objectives of the Charter Organization, except with respect to youth development, consistent with the goals and objectives of the Scouting program."*

**[DEFINITIVE]** — Relevant if the pack's chartering organization wants its own logo alongside Scouting marks on the website.

## 2. International Level — WOSM and the World Scout Emblem

### 2.1 What the World Scout Emblem Is

The World Scout Emblem is the purple-and-white fleur-de-lis-in-a-rope-circle mark of the **World Organization of the Scout Movement (WOSM)**. It is distinct from Scouting America's own fleur-de-lis marks. Its application is governed by **WOSM Conference Resolution 9/1969** and the **World Scout Brand Manual**. ([WOSM World Scout Brand Short Identity Guide](https://learn.scout.org/resource/world-scout-brand-short-identity-guide); [WOSM support article](https://support.scout.org/hc/en-gb/articles/10382652904335-How-should-the-redesigned-World-Scout-Emblem-and-the-new-World-Scouting-logo-be-used)) **[STRONG]**

WOSM refreshed its brand system ("Ready for Life") and introduced a separate **World Scouting logo**, which National Scout Organizations use to signal their membership in the global movement. ([WOSM news](https://www.scout.org/news/news/ready-life-scoutings-new-brand-welcomes-new-era)) **[STRONG]**

### 2.2 How WOSM Rights Flow Down — Scouting America's Own Answer

The cleanest primary source here is **Scouting America's own published guidance**, not WOSM's, because it states exactly how far the entitlement reaches inside the United States. From *World Scout Emblem Usage*, published by Scouting America:

> *"The World Scout Emblem is the distinctive symbol of the World Organization of the Scout Movement (WOSM) and is worn by Scouts worldwide. It is also a protected symbol and is, therefore, subject to licensing restrictions."*

> **Non-Commercial Use:** *"By virtue of our membership of WOSM, **the Boy Scouts of America** is entitled to use the World Scout Emblem for non-commercial purposes. Non-commercial use of the World Scout Emblem covers those cases where the items are not offered for sale. Examples of non-commercial use would include educational material, training material, general management materials, publications, or other promotional material. Such applications will not require a non-commercial license.*
>
> *However, when the non-commercial use of the World Scout Emblem is used on products, such as listed below, a specific non-commercial license must be obtained in advance. All applications for non-commercial licenses should be submitted to the World Scout Bureau Central Office:*
> - *clothing, badges, leather goods, metal pin badges*
> - *flags, tents, rucksacks, camping, outdoor and adventure equipment*
> - *computer hardware and software"*

> **Commercial Use:** *"…it is the act of 'offering for sale' that defines this as a commercial use… The use of the World Scout Emblem on any item being offered for sale is subject to obtaining a unique product specific commercial license for each item. Royalties of 5% calculated on sales value will be collected on all items bearing the World Scout Emblem."*

> *"Please contact the International Department at international@scouting.org or 972-580-2403 for any outstanding questions or clarifications."*

— [World Scout Emblem Usage, Scouting America](https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/World-Scout-Emblem-Usage.pdf) (live URL blocks automated fetching; retrieved via [Internet Archive](https://web.archive.org/web/2020/https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/World-Scout-Emblem-Usage.pdf)) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

### 2.3 What This Means for Pack 170

Read the grant carefully: the entitlement runs to **"the Boy Scouts of America"** — the National Scout Organization — not to its units. Nothing in the document delegates that entitlement downward to a pack acting on its own initiative.

And Scouting America's custom-patch rules close the door explicitly. Among the categories a licensee **must not produce** for a council or unit:

> *"Patches depicting the World Scouting Crest"*

— [Guidelines for Custom Patches and Emblems, p.15](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/33066/33066_Guidelines_for_Custom_Patches_And_Emblems.pdf) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

**Conclusion: Pack 170 should not use the World Scout Emblem anywhere — not on the website, not on shirts, not on a pack patch.** It is not a Cub Scout program mark, it adds nothing to a pack's identity, and its use sits outside the authority a charter conveys. This is the one area where the answer is a clean, unqualified no. **[DEFINITIVE]**

*(The World Scout Emblem legitimately appears on the official Scouts BSA uniform as the World Crest patch, purchased through the Scout Shop. That is Supply Group merchandise, already licensed. Wearing it is fine; reproducing it is not.)*

### 3.2 The Core Ownership Rule

Identical language appears in both the legacy and the 2024 guidelines:

> *"The trademarks and logos of Scouting America are protected by a 1916 act of Congress (36 U.S.C. 27) as well as by a variety of registrations with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office. The 1916 act specifically gives Scouting America the sole and exclusive right to use emblems, badges, descriptive or designating marks, and words or phrases the corporation adopts.*
>
> *These and all art or logotypes obtained from the Scouting America National Council are the exclusive property of Scouting America and **must be used and displayed as shown in this manual or official artwork** unless otherwise stated in writing from an authorized officer of the Scouting America National Council. In other words, they must appear with any ownership symbols exactly as received, and no additional symbols are to appear in connection with them."*

> *"Scouting America maintains its right to regulate use of trademarks and constrain that use whenever the national organization, in its sole discretion, deems it necessary to do so."*

— [Scouting America Brand Guidelines 2024, p.34](https://pathwaytoadventure.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Scouting-America-Brand-Guidelines-2024-BC.pdf); [BSA Brand Guidelines, p.14](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/310-0231.pdf) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

**Read this precisely.** The rule is *not* "you may not use the marks." It is **"art obtained from the National Council must be used exactly as provided."** That framing presupposes that people within the Scouting community *do* obtain and use the art — which is exactly what the Brand Center exists to enable. The prohibition is on **modification**, not on **use**.

### 3.3 The Attribution Requirement

> *"An attribution statement must be placed at the bottom of any advertisement or poster that clearly identifies trademarks or design marks of Scouting America. This might read as follows: 'BE PREPARED is a registered trademark of Scouting America.'"*

**[DEFINITIVE]** — Actionable for the pack site: put an attribution line in the footer.

Also required: *"the proper statutory symbol (®, ™, or ©) is properly affixed to trademarks used in communications."* The ® goes at the lower right of a design mark, at approximately one-third the size of the largest element, *"but never so small that it can't be read."* **[DEFINITIVE]**

### 3.4 The Cub Scouts Program Trademark — Exact Rules

> *"The Cub Scouts trademark consists of the wolf, the words 'Cub Scouts,' and a fleur-de-lis."*

**Five acceptable reproductions:** One Color · Spot Color · Four Color (CMYK) · Screen Usage · Reversed. Each must carry the ®.

| Reproduction | Specification |
|---|---|
| One Color | Black or any dark color may be used |
| Spot Color | Blue: **PMS 294** · Yellow: **PMS 116** |
| Four Color (CMYK) | Blue: **100, 48, 0, 44** · Yellow: **0, 10, 100, 0** |
| **Screen Usage (web)** | Blue: **`#003F87`** · Yellow: **`#FCD116`** |
| Reversed | White must be used |

**Four explicitly prohibited treatments, quoted verbatim:**

1. *"Do not reproduce in a tint or screen."*
2. *"Do not alter the signature in any way, including changing the typeface or colors."*
3. *"Do not add effects, including a drop shadow, bevel, or glow."*
4. **"Do not use the Wolf element outside of the approved trademark."**

— [Scouting America Brand Guidelines 2024, p.22](https://pathwaytoadventure.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Scouting-America-Brand-Guidelines-2024-BC.pdf) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

> **This settles an open question from the prior research document.** `#003F87` and `#FCD116` are not third-party guesses from brand-color aggregator sites — they are printed in the official 2024 Scouting America Brand Guidelines as the **Screen Usage** values for the Cub Scouts trademark. Use them with confidence. **[DEFINITIVE]**

> **This also rules out a tempting design move.** Isolating the Wolf head as a cute standalone pack mascot icon is specifically and explicitly prohibited. **[DEFINITIVE]**

### 3.5 The Fleur-de-lis as an Icon — Affirmatively Permitted

From the legacy Brand Guidelines, Icons section (p.59):

> *"Scouting is rich in iconography. Every badge has its own visual meaning: council badges, rank badges, and merit badges are all linked to the look of Scouting… It can be tempting to create new icons for digital projects, but with few exceptions, these distract and sometimes create conflict with existing BSA-approved icons.*
>
> ***As a rule, avoid creating original icons. Instead, use approved Boy Scouts of America icons (e.g., the fleur-de-lis as an app icon).***
>
> *You may use universally recognized third-party social media icons (make sure you comply with the license holder's terms of service)."*

— [BSA Brand Guidelines, p.59](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/310-0231.pdf) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

**This is the most useful finding in this document for the website build.** National brand policy does not merely tolerate the fleur-de-lis in digital contexts — it **recommends** it, and simultaneously **discourages inventing original Scouting-flavored iconography**. That is the opposite of the cautious "use generic compass-rose imagery instead" instinct in the prior research file. For a favicon, an app icon, or a section marker, official Scouting artwork is the *preferred* route. **[DEFINITIVE]**

### 3.6 The Brand Center — Where Units Get Legitimate Artwork

The **Brand Center** ([scoutingwire.org/BSA-Brand-Center/](https://scoutingwire.org/BSA-Brand-Center/), asset library at `scouting.webdamdb.com`) is the official DAM. It is **free**, requires **registration**, and is explicitly unit-facing:

> *"Thousands of professionally designed unit marketing tools… from flyers and social media graphics to banners and videos, and it's easy to search, download, and **customize materials for your pack, troop, or post**."*

Available: **BSA/Scouting America logos** in multiple file formats, program logos (Cub Scouts, Scouts BSA, Venturing, Sea Scouts, Exploring), photo library, videos, social media images, web banners, email templates, postcards, yard signs, and **Dynamic Templates** (customizable, login-gated).

— [Scouting Wire Brand Center](https://scoutingwire.org/BSA-Brand-Center/); [BSA Brand Center… What's in it? (official PDF)](https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/BSA-Brand-Center%E2%80%A6Whats-in-it.pdf); [nmscouts.org unit walkthrough](https://www.nmscouts.org/post/how-to-use-the-bsa-brand-center-your-unit-s-free-marketing-hub) **[STRONG — official secondary]**

**Practical takeaway: this is where Pack 170's logo files come from.** Not Google Images, not a traced SVG, not a Wikipedia file. Register, download the official Cub Scouts trademark files, and use them as-is. **[STRONG]**

> **Is there an official "unit logo builder" that generates a Pack-170-specific lockup?** **No — searched extensively, none found.** The Brand Center's Dynamic Templates let a unit drop its number into pre-designed *flyers and recruitment materials*, but there is no official tool producing a custom unit logo. Custom **unit numerals** exist only as physical uniform insignia sold through [scoutshop.org](https://www.scoutshop.org/new-insignia/patches-and-badges/custom-numerals.html) and licensed vendors, not as a branding system. **[DEFINITIVE — negative finding]**

### 3.7 Third-Party Trademarks and Modification

> *"No third party trademarks (including GSUSA) will be authorized without prior written authorization expressed from the trademark owner (on company letter), and written approval from the BSA Licensing Team."*

> **Modified Trademark Use:** *"The BSA understands that in some cases, reproduction of certain elements of trademarks and logos in the embroidery process may be substandard due to the size and the reproduction capabilities of these elements… If modifications are made to the BSA trademarks, they require permission from the BSA Licensing Team and are subject to design approval on a case-by-case basis. **No third party can lay claim to any designs incorporating any elements of the BSA trademarks.**"*

That last sentence matters: **Pack 170 cannot claim ownership of any pack logo it creates that incorporates Scouting marks.** A "Pack 170 logo" built around the fleur-de-lis is Scouting America's property, not the pack's.

## 5. Websites — The Specific Answer

### 5.1 Does National Policy Apply to Unit Websites?

The legacy Brand Guidelines' **Web Policies** section (p.43) opens:

> *"Maintaining the Boy Scouts of America brand image online goes beyond clean design and simple navigation. **While these guidelines apply to council sites, unit sites can also benefit from these guidelines.**"*

— [BSA Brand Guidelines, p.43](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/310-0231.pdf) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

**Read this precisely too.** The web policies are written as **mandatory for councils and advisory for units** ("can also benefit"). This is the single most important sentence for the pack website, and it cuts in the pack's favor: national policy explicitly contemplates unit websites, does not prohibit them, and does not impose the council rulebook on them. **[DEFINITIVE]**

That said, three of the council rules are effectively mandatory for a pack anyway, because they restate obligations that come from elsewhere: the youth-privacy rule (Youth Protection), the intellectual-property rule (trademark law and the charter agreement), and the image-permission rule (Youth Protection).

### 5.3 Can the Pack Website Display the Cub Scouts Logo?

**Yes — provided the artwork is official, obtained from the Brand Center, displayed unmodified, in one of the five approved reproductions, with the ® symbol.** **[STRONG — official secondary]**

The reasoning chain, each link sourced:

1. The Brand Center exists to distribute logo files and templates and states they are for units to *"search, download, and customize… for your pack, troop, or post."* ([Scouting Wire](https://scoutingwire.org/BSA-Brand-Center/))
2. The ownership rule prohibits **modification**, not use: art *"must be used and displayed as shown in this manual or official artwork."* ([2024 Guidelines p.34](https://pathwaytoadventure.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Scouting-America-Brand-Guidelines-2024-BC.pdf))
3. Web policies contemplate unit sites rather than prohibiting them. ([Legacy Guidelines p.43](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/310-0231.pdf))
4. The charter agreement obligates the unit to follow brand guidelines — which presupposes the unit is using the brand. ([Charter Agreement §III.B.1](https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Fillable-Annual-Charter-Agreement-Charter-Orgs_Short-Version_09.16.2022.pdf))
5. The **licensed-vendor rule is scoped to physical products** — *"on any product, including patches, pins, and T-shirts"* — and to the **manufacturer**. It says nothing about digital display. ([2024 Guidelines p.33](https://pathwaytoadventure.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Scouting-America-Brand-Guidelines-2024-BC.pdf))

**Official position vs. common practice:** No single official sentence reads "a pack may put the Cub Scouts logo on its website." The conclusion is a strong inference from five converging primary sources plus a purpose-built national tool that hands units the files. In common practice this is **universal** — essentially every pack and troop website in the country displays program logos, and there is no known instance of enforcement against a unit for doing so. The gap between the official position and common practice here is small and low-risk.

### 5.4 Can the Site Display the Fleur-de-lis?

**Yes, and it is the recommended choice for icons.** See §3.5 — the guidelines affirmatively instruct: *"As a rule, avoid creating original icons. Instead, use approved Boy Scouts of America icons (e.g., the fleur-de-lis as an app icon)."* **[DEFINITIVE]**

**Caveat:** use the **Scouting America fleur-de-lis**, not the WOSM World Scout Emblem (§2.3). And note the trademark page's careful scoping — the fleur-de-lis is protected *"when used in conjunction with a Scouting activity."* Since a pack site is inherently a Scouting activity, the pack is using a protected mark, under charter authority, and must use official artwork. **[DEFINITIVE]**

### 5.5 Can the Site Display Rank Badge Images (Lion, Tiger, Wolf, Bear, Webelos, Arrow of Light)?

**Most likely yes for display, definitively no for manufacture. This is the clearest gray area.** **[INFERRED — verify with council if it matters]**

- **Definitively prohibited:** producing *"Rank insignia, or patches/emblems substantially similar to rank insignia."* ([Custom Patches Guidelines p.15](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/33066/33066_Guidelines_for_Custom_Patches_And_Emblems.pdf)) **[DEFINITIVE]**
- **Not prohibited anywhere found:** *displaying* official rank badge artwork in a website, flyer, or newsletter. The Brand Guidelines' Icons section even refers to *"council badges, rank badges, and merit badges"* as part of the approved icon vocabulary rather than as forbidden imagery. **[STRONG]**
- **The distinction that governs:** the patch rules regulate **manufacturing**; the brand rules regulate **display and modification**. A rank badge image on a webpage is display, not manufacture.
- **Do not:** redraw, recolor, or stylize rank badges; use them to imply a Scout holds a rank they don't; or reproduce Cub Scout Handbook rank-requirement pages (that would violate *"cannot replicate any BSA publication currently for sale through the Supply Group"*). **[STRONG]**
- **Safest source:** rank badge imagery from the Brand Center or official Scouting America program pages.

### 5.6 A Custom "Pack 170" Logo — Where the Line Sits

There is **no official unit-lockup system** (§3.6). Applying the modification rule:

| Approach | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Official Cub Scouts trademark, unmodified, **placed adjacent to** separately-set type reading "Pack 170 · Macon, Georgia" | **Safe.** The mark is unmodified; the pack identity is typographic and separate. **[STRONG]** |
| Fleur-de-lis used as a favicon / app icon | **Safe and affirmatively recommended.** **[DEFINITIVE]** |

| Original "Scouting-flavored" iconography (compass roses, trail markers) invented for the site | **Legally safest, but contrary to brand guidance** — *"As a rule, avoid creating original icons."* **[DEFINITIVE that it's discouraged]** |

> **This reverses a recommendation in the prior research document**, which suggested using generic scouting-style iconography instead of official marks as the "legally safer" path. Legally safer, yes — but it is the option national brand policy explicitly tells you to avoid. Using the real, unmodified marks from the Brand Center is both compliant and better-looking.

## 6. Merchandise & Print (T-Shirts, Banners, Flyers)

### 6.1 The Licensed-Vendor Rule — The Single Most Important Practical Rule

Identical in the legacy and 2024 editions:

> **Branded Products**
>
> *"Any use of Scouting America's trademarks by any third party on any product, including patches, pins, and T-shirts, requires that the manufacturer of these products be licensed by the Scouting America National Council."*

> **Patches**
>
> *"Scouting America requires that all patches be manufactured by the Supply Group or by an official Scouting America licensee.*
>
> *Scouting America reviews each request for embroidered use of all brand trademarks as submitted by its licensees. Licensees will facilitate all authorizations with Scouting America. **Any trademark that is used on a patch not created by the Supply Group or by an official Scouting America licensee is considered an unauthorized use of Scouting America's trademarks.**"*

— [Scouting America Brand Guidelines 2024, p.33](https://pathwaytoadventure.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Scouting-America-Brand-Guidelines-2024-BC.pdf); [BSA Brand Guidelines, p.26](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/310-0231.pdf) **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

**Note precisely where the obligation lands: on the *manufacturer*.** This resolves the "is a chartered unit a third party?" gray area as a practical matter — it doesn't matter. Whether or not Pack 170 is a "third party," the **printer must be licensed**. The pack's compliance obligation is therefore simple and checkable: *pick a vendor from the list.*

Reinforced by [licensingbsa.org](https://licensingbsa.org/about/about-official-scouting-america-products/): *"any other product(s) manufactured by a third party that desires to use any trademarks proprietary to the Scouting America must be licensed to do so,"* and *"**Unauthorized products, such as t-shirts, patches and promotional items, may be illegal if produced without authorization.**"* **[DEFINITIVE]**

### 6.3 Custom Pack Patches — The Detailed Rules

If Pack 170 wants a custom patch (pack anniversary, day-camp, pinewood derby), these rules govern. All from [Guidelines for Custom Patches and Emblems, pp.15–17](https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/33066/33066_Guidelines_for_Custom_Patches_And_Emblems.pdf). **[DEFINITIVE — primary source]**

**How to order:**
> *"Local councils and units can work directly through the National Supply Group or through official licensees who are authorized to produce custom patches and emblems, which can be found at www.licensingbsa.org."*

**Requirements — every custom patch must:**
- **Carry a Scouting America corporate identifier:** *"All temporary patches and emblems must contain a Boy Scouts of America corporate identifier: either 'BSA' without any periods, 'Boy Scouts of America,' or the BSA fleur-de-lis."*
- **Be tied to an event:** *"All patches and emblems must be for a Scouting event or activity."* And specifically: *"**Unit**, district, and council emblems must be for a specific event or activity such as a camporee or summer camp."*
  - ⚠️ **This means a generic "Pack 170" patch with no event tie is not permitted.** Tie it to an event — "Pack 170 Fall Campout 2026," "Pack 170 Pinewood Derby." **[DEFINITIVE]**
- **Come from a licensed supplier:** *"All third party suppliers of custom embroidered patches and emblems are required to be licensed with the BSA. All officially licensed custom patches and emblems will have an 'officially licensed' backing or a holographic sticker affixed to it."*

**A licensee must not produce:**
- Council shoulder patches currently stocked by Supply Group, or any patch in a CSP shape that isn't a CSP
- Generic or "stock" patches already sold by Supply Group
- **Rank insignia, or patches/emblems substantially similar to rank insignia**
- Patrol emblems and badges of office currently stocked by Supply Group
- **Merit badges or merit badge "knockoffs"**
- *"Parody patches or emblems that the BSA, at its sole discretion, would deem as reflecting poorly on the BSA or its programs"*
- Patches containing the **Confederate flag** (except within an existing, currently-used state flag)
- **Patches depicting the World Scouting Crest**
- Patches containing the **Olympic rings** or the word "Olympics"
- Patches containing **Exploring or Learning for Life** logos alongside other BSA trademarks

**Additional restrictions:**
- *"No third party trademarks (including GSUSA) will be authorized without prior written authorization expressed from the trademark owner (on company letter), and written approval from the BSA Licensing Team."*
- *"No text or designs over the BSA universal emblem, when used"*
- *"No text or designs superimposed over the American flag."* A **stationary** American flag may not be used as patch backing; a **"waving"** flag may.
- *"No obscene gestures or foul language"*
- *"No words or designs that the BSA would consider, at its sole discretion, to reflect poorly on the values of the BSA or that it would deem offensive"*
- *"There should not be any direct political reference made on patches."*
- *"Any use of American Indian imagery must adhere to BSA American Indian guidelines."*
- **Statutory markings:** *"BSA patches and emblems do not require statutory markings (i.e., ®, ™, ©), except in cases where the emblem or patch is of sufficient size that these marks can be produced legibly. Typically this is a patch that is 3½ inches in diameter or larger for a round patch, or any patch that is at least 4 inches wide or tall."*

**Modification:** *"If modifications are made to the BSA trademarks, they require permission from the BSA Licensing Team and are subject to design approval on a case-by-case basis. No third party can lay claim to any designs incorporating any elements of the BSA trademarks."*

## 7. Recommended Policy for Pack 170

A short, adoptable policy. Each line traces to a source above.

### Do

1. **Get all Scouting artwork from the Brand Center** ([scoutingwire.org/BSA-Brand-Center/](https://scoutingwire.org/BSA-Brand-Center/)). Register a pack account. Never source logos from Google Images, Wikipedia, or a traced SVG.
2. **Use the Cub Scouts trademark unmodified** in one of the five approved reproductions. For web, Screen Usage: `#003F87` / `#FCD116`.
3. **Use the Scouting America fleur-de-lis for icons and favicon** — brand policy recommends it and discourages inventing alternatives.
4. **Anchor the palette on Cub Scout Blue `#003F87` and Gold `#FCD116`.** Primary-source confirmed.
5. **Put an attribution line and copyright notice in the footer.** Something like: *"Cub Scouts® and the fleur-de-lis are registered trademarks of Scouting America, used by Cub Scout Pack 170 under charter. Pack 170 is chartered to [organization] and served by the Central Georgia Council. This site is not an official communication channel of Scouting America or the Central Georgia Council."* — this discharges the attribution requirement and the non-endorsement disclaimer in one block.
6. **Publish clear contact information on every page** (Required Elements, p.73).
7. **Name youth by first name and last initial only.** No exceptions.
8. **Order all shirts, patches, and banners from a licensed vendor** — check [licensingbsa.org/scouting-america-licensee-list/](https://licensingbsa.org/scouting-america-licensee-list/) before every order.
9. **Tie any custom pack patch to a specific event** and include a corporate identifier ("BSA," "Boy Scouts of America," or the fleur-de-lis).
10. **Buy uniform insignia and numerals at the Central Georgia Scout Shop** in Macon.
11. **Run at least two adult administrators**, one a YPT-trained registered leader, on the site and every social account. Keep everything public.
12. **Hold the domain and hosting in a way that reflects chartered-organization ownership**, not a single volunteer's personal account.
13. **Route media inquiries to the Scout Executive** at the Central Georgia Council.

### Don't

1. **Don't modify any Scouting mark** — no recolor, retypeset, tint, screen, drop shadow, bevel, or glow.
2. **Don't extract the Wolf element** from the Cub Scouts trademark.
3. **Don't use the World Scout Emblem** anywhere.
4. **Don't use an unlicensed printer** for anything bearing a Scouting mark.
5. **Don't make anything resembling rank insignia, merit badges, patrol emblems, or badges of office.**
6. **Don't reproduce Supply Group publications** — no scanned handbook pages or rank requirement pages. Link to official sources instead.
7. **Don't run ads or sell Scouting merchandise** on the site. Avoid ad-injecting free hosting tiers.
8. **Don't build private groups, DM features, or any one-on-one adult–youth channel.**
9. **Don't claim to be an official Scouting America or Central Georgia Council site.**
10. **Don't assume Pack 170 owns any logo it creates around Scouting marks** — it doesn't.
